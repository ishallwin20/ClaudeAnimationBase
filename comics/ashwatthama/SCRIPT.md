# CHIRANJEEVI · Issue 1 · ASHWATTHAMA: *The Night He Didn't Sleep*

> Page numbers here are the first draft's. The comic now runs to 44 pages: Day 15 (p7), Kripa's counsel (p15) and the
> Ganga (p29) were each split into two pages for clarity (owner, 14 Nov). `comic.js` lists the pages in order.

Season plan: [CHIRANJEEVI.md](../../CHIRANJEEVI.md). Swipe pages are 4:5 (1080 × 1350 design units, made at 2160 × 2700).
Every beat below comes from the Mahabharata, critical edition (BORI; text from GRETIL), cited as book.chapter.verse.
Ganguli's translation (Gutenberg) follows the vulgate, so his section numbers differ; the verses were matched in Sanskrit.

**The idea of the issue.** A man born with a gem that kept hunger, disease and fear away from him. On one night, grief
for his father and for his king turns into a plan he learnt from an owl. By morning he has broken every rule of war,
and the curse he walks away with is the exact opposite of the gem: alone, unable to talk to anyone, carrying every
disease. The issue doesn't excuse him or sneer at him. It lets the reader watch it happen.

**Tone.** Night, cold blue and black; fire orange for the camp; gold only for the gem and for the gods. Violence
happens off panel or in silhouette: we show the start of the act and the aftermath, never the act itself (18+ reader,
but nothing gratuitous).

**Text.** Narration boxes (short, present tense of the story) and speech balloons. Lines in quotes are close
paraphrases of the verse cited; nothing a character says goes beyond what the text gives them.

**Motif.** The gem glows faintly in every panel he is in. On the last pages it is gone, and the panels around him have
no gold at all.

## Cover (p1)

Ashwatthama from the chest up, head bowed, eyes up at us; the gem on his forehead the only bright thing; behind him
the banyan full of crows, the owl's two eyes in it. Title: **ASHWATTHAMA**; above it, small, **CHIRANJEEVI · 1**;
below, *The night he didn't sleep*.

## Cold open (p2–3)

**p2.** A forest at night, now or any century. A cloaked figure walks away from us between the trees, alone.
- Box: *There is a prayer Hindus have recited for centuries. It names eight people who never die.*
- Box (Devanagari, small, gold): अश्वत्थामा बलिर्व्यासो हनूमांश्च विभीषणः …
- Box: *The first name in it is his.*

**p3.** Splash. The figure turns his head; we see only a cloth wound round his forehead and one eye.
- Title lettering: **ASHWATTHAMA**
- Box: *This is how it began.*

## Act 1: Who he was (p4–9)

**p4. The birth.** (Adi 1.121.11–14)
- P1: Drona's ashram hut at dawn; Kripi (his mother) holding a newborn.
- P2: The baby's mouth open in a huge cry. SFX (horse-like, lettered as a neigh): **HIIIEEEHH!**
- P3: Drona looks up: a voice from the sky. Balloon (sky, no tail, a jagged gold border): *"His cry carried to every
  direction, like the horse Uchchaihshravas. Let him be called ASHWATTHAMA."*
- Box: *Ashwa: horse. The boy who neighed at birth.*

**p5. The gem.** (Sauptika 10.11.20 "sahaja maṇi"; 10.15.28–30)
- P1: Close on the baby's forehead: a small gem, part of him. Box: *He was born with a gem on his forehead.*
- P2–3: Three small panels in a row, the grown man walking untouched through each: arrows breaking off him; a famine
  village; a fever ward. Boxes: *No weapon.* / *No hunger.* / *No disease.*
- Box (bottom): *"Whoever wears it has nothing to fear from gods, demons or nagas," he would later say.* (10.15.29)

**p6. Drona's son.** (Adi 1.121.15–16; the son of the Kauravas' teacher)
- One wide panel: Drona teaching the Kuru princes archery; a young Ashwatthama beside his father, already better
  armed than the princes. Box: *His father, Drona, taught every prince of the Kurus. The son grew up on the
  battlefield's side of the war.*

**p7. Day 15 of Kurukshetra: the lie.** (Drona 7.164.68–73)
- P1: Krishna's advice, in a tight two-shot with the Pandavas. Balloon (Krishna): *"Put dharma aside to win. If
  Ashwatthama falls, Drona won't fight. Let someone tell him so."* (164.68–69) Box: *Arjuna refused.* (164.70)
- P2: Bhima's mace coming down on an elephant (off panel; we see the swing and the dust).
  Box: *Bhima killed an elephant. Its name was Ashwatthama.* (164.71)
- P3: Bhima shouting across the field, ashamed (savrīḍam). Big balloon: **"ASHWATTHAMA IS DEAD!"** (164.72)

**p8. The truthful king.** (Drona 7.164.104–109; 7.165.46–52)
- P1: Drona turns to Yudhishthira, the one man who never lies. Balloon: *"Is it true?"*
- P2: Yudhishthira, eyes down: *"Ashwatthama is dead."* Then, small and faint, a whisper balloon: *(…the elephant.)*
  (164.106 "avyaktam … hataḥ kuñjara")
- P3: Yudhishthira's chariot, which always ran four fingers above the ground, touches the earth. (164.107)
- P4: Drona lays down his bow. (164.108–109) Dhrishtadyumna climbs onto his chariot, sword raised; across the field
  Arjuna shouting *"Bring him alive! Don't kill him!"* (165.50–52). The panel cuts before the stroke.

**p9.** Silence page. One panel: Ashwatthama far across the field, hearing. No text. Then a small box at the bottom:
*He did not forget the name Dhrishtadyumna.*

## Act 2: The last night (p10–27)

**p10. Day 18.** (Shalya 9.64.38–40)
- P1: Duryodhana on the ground by the lake, his thighs broken, at dusk.
- P2: Kripa pouring water from a pot over Ashwatthama's head. Box: *Dying, Duryodhana made him commander of an army that
  no longer existed.* (64.40 "drauṇiṃ … senāpatye 'bhyaṣecayat")
- P3: Three silhouettes riding away into the dark. Box: *Three men were left: Ashwatthama, his uncle Kripa, and
  Kritavarma.* (Sauptika 10.1.1–6)

**p11. Under the banyan.** (10.1.18–33) [STYLE TEST]
- P1 (wide): a vast banyan against the moon; three figures at its foot. Box: *They hid in a forest and lay down under a
  great banyan.*
- P2: Kripa and Kritavarma asleep on the bare earth. Box: *Men who had always slept on costly beds slept on the
  ground.* (1.31)
- P3: Ashwatthama's face in the dark, eyes open, the gem faintly lit. Box: *Drona's son could not sleep. He lay
  hissing like a snake, burning with rage.* (1.32–33)

**p12. The crows.** (10.1.34–37) [STYLE TEST]
- P1 (tall): the banyan's branches, thousands of crows asleep, each on its own perch. Box: *In that banyan, thousands of
  crows slept, safe.* (1.35)
- P2: Ashwatthama looking up. No text.
- P3: Two eyes in the dark, beyond the branches. Box: *Then he saw it coming.* (1.36)

**p13. The owl.** (10.1.37–41) [STYLE TEST]
- P1 (splash, two thirds): the owl, huge, tawny, yellow-green eyes, wings spread, dropping into the branches. Box: *A
  huge owl, terrible to look at, fast as Garuda.* (1.37) Small SFX: *hoo…* (1.38: it made a soft sound)
- P2: talons closing round a sleeping crow. No text.
- P3: black feathers falling through the moonlight. Box: *It tore off wings. It cut off heads. It broke legs. It killed
  every crow it could see.* (1.40–41)

**p14. The lesson.** (10.1.42–52) [STYLE TEST]
- P1: extreme close-up of his eye; the owl reflected in it.
- P2: his face, fierce, the gem bright. Thought balloon: *"This bird has just taught me how to fight."* (1.44)
- P3: the owl alone on a branch above the dead. Thought balloon: *"In a fair fight the Pandavas will kill me. But
  asleep, at night…"* (1.45–46, 1.51–52)

**p15. He wakes them.** (10.1.54–55; chapters 2–5)
- P1: He shakes Kripa and Kritavarma awake and tells them. Box: *They were too ashamed to answer.* (1.55)
- P2: Kripa, gently: *"Wait for morning. Ask Dhritarashtra, Gandhari, Vidura. Then decide."* (chapter 2)
- P3: Ashwatthama: *"The man who killed my father is asleep in that camp. I will not sleep until he is dead."*
  (chapters 3–4: he cannot rest "until I have killed Dhrishtadyumna")
- P4: Box: *Kripa told him killing sleeping men is a sin that will never wash off. He went anyway, and they followed.*
  (chapter 5)

**p16–17. The guardian at the gate.** (10.6.1–14)
- p16 splash: at the camp gate stands a being "bright as the sun and moon", in a tiger skin dripping blood, a black
  antelope skin over his shoulder, a great snake for a sacred thread, many arms raised with weapons, a mouth of flames,
  covered in a thousand eyes; and out of the flames pour hundreds and thousands of Krishnas with conch, discus and mace.
  (6.3–9) Box: *Something was guarding the gate.*
- p17: Four panels: his arrows swallowed (6.11); his spear shatters (6.12–13); his sword dissolves into the being
  "like a tuft of cotton" (6.14–15; Ganguli's vulgate has "like a mongoose into its hole"); his mace swallowed (6.16).
  Last panel: the sky full of Krishnas; he has nothing left in his hands (6.17). Thought: *"I didn't listen to Kripa."*
  (6.18–)

**p18–19. The offering.** (10.7.1–66)
- p18: He kneels and praises Shiva by name after name (7.2–10). A golden fire altar appears. Strange beings crowd round
  (7.12–50, shown as silhouettes in the dark). He climbs into the fire: *"I offer myself to you. Take me, if I cannot
  beat them."* (7.54–58)
- p19 P1: Shiva appears, half smiling (7.59). Balloon: *"Krishna has worshipped me in truth, purity and devotion; no one
  is dearer to me. For him I protected the Panchalas."* (7.60–63)
- p19 P2: Balloon: *"But time has overtaken them. Their lives are over tonight."* (7.63 "abhibhūtās tu kālena")
- p19 P3: Shiva gives him a sword and enters his body. He blazes. (7.64–65) Box: *Invisible beings ran with him into the
  camp.* (7.66)

**p20–21. Dhrishtadyumna.** (10.8.10–21)
- p20 P1: He enters the camp where there is no gate (8.9), softly, toward one tent (8.10).
- p20 P2: Dhrishtadyumna asleep on white silk, flowers, incense (8.12–13).
- p20 P3: A foot wakes him (8.14). His eyes open: he knows who it is (8.15).
- p21 P1: Dhrishtadyumna, held down: *"Son of my teacher, kill me with a weapon. Don't delay. Let me go to the worlds
  of the brave."* (8.19)
- p21 P2: Ashwatthama: *"There are no worlds for men who kill their teacher. You don't deserve a weapon."* (8.20)
- p21 P3: Black panel. (8.21: the killing is not shown.)

**p22–24. The night.** (10.8.22–104)
- p22: The tent's women and guards frozen in terror: *"Is it a rakshasa or a man?"* (8.29–30) Silhouettes of warriors
  waking, falling to the rudrastra (8.31).
- p23: Wide: the camp in silhouette, one figure "like Death sent by Time" moving through it (8.39). Draupadi's five
  sons wake and fight (8.42–58): show five bows raised against one figure, then five dropped bows.
- p24 P1: At the gate, Kripa and Kritavarma cut down everyone who runs out (8.100–102).
- p24 P2: They set the camp on fire in three places (8.103).
- p24 P3: In the firelight he moves "like a practised hand" (8.104). Box: *By dawn, the camp was silent.*

**p25. Duryodhana.** (10.9.50–55)
- P1: Dawn by the lake. The three kneel by the dying king. Ashwatthama: *"The Pandavas have no sons now. Their camp is
  gone. I killed Dhrishtadyumna like an animal."* (9.50–51)
- P2: Duryodhana, almost smiling: *"Not Bhishma, not Karna, not your father did for me what you did tonight."* (9.53)
- P3: *"Farewell. We will meet in heaven."* He dies. (9.55)

**p26. Draupadi.** (10.11.14–20)
- P1: Draupadi among the dead, her five sons.
- P2: To Yudhishthira: *"If he isn't punished, I will sit here and fast until I die."* (11.14–15)
- P3: *"He was born with a gem on his head. Bring it to me."* (11.20)

**p27. The chase.** (10.11.21–30; 10.13.1–14)
- P1: Bhima's chariot racing out alone (11.28–30).
- P2: Krishna, Arjuna and Yudhishthira follow on Krishna's chariot, Garuda on its flag (13.1–9).

## Act 3: The weapon and the curse (p28–38)

**p28. Flashback: the discus.** (10.12.11–38)
- Krishna, on the chariot: *"He once came to me in Dwaraka and offered me the Brahmashira for my discus."* (12.11–15)
- P2: Young Ashwatthama gripping the Sudarshana with his left hand, then his right, then with all his strength. It
  doesn't move. (12.21–23)
- P3: Krishna: *"He asked for the one thing no one else has asked me for. He is reckless, and he knows that weapon."*
  (12.24–38)

**p29. On the bank of the Ganga.** (10.13.15–20)
- P1: Vyasa among rishis by the river; Ashwatthama beside them, in a kusha-grass cloth, smeared with ghee, covered in
  dust (13.15–16).
- P2: Bhima, bow drawn: *"Stop! Stop!"* (13.15)
- P3: He picks up a blade of grass with his left hand (13.17).
- P4: Close on his mouth: **"APANDAVAYA — for a world without Pandavas."** (13.18)
- P5: The grass bursts into fire, "like Yama at the end of the age" (13.20).

**p30. Arjuna.** (10.14.1–6)
- P1: Krishna: *"Arjuna, now. The weapon Drona taught you."* (14.1–4)
- P2: Arjuna steps down, bows to the gods and his elders, and says *"Peace to my teacher's son. Peace to me. Peace to my
  brothers."* (14.5–6)
- P3: He releases it: *"Let this weapon quiet that weapon."* (14.6)

**p31. Splash: two Brahmastras.** (10.14.7–10) Two suns of fire facing each other across the river; meteors falling;
the earth shaking with its mountains and trees. No text.

**p32. Two sages.** (10.14.11–15)
- P1: Narada and Vyasa standing *between* the two fires, blazing like fires themselves.
- P2: *"The great warriors who died here knew such weapons. Not one of them ever used it on human beings. What have you
  done?"* (14.16)

**p33. One can take it back. One can't.** (10.15.1–12)
- P1: Arjuna withdraws his weapon (15.1–3). Box: *Only a man who had kept his vows could call it back.* (15.6–9)
- P2: Ashwatthama tries. It won't come. (15.10–12)
- P3: *"I can't. I let it go for the destruction of the Pandavas."* (15.12–17)

**p34. The gem.** (10.15.27–33)
- P1: Vyasa: *"Give them the gem on your head, and they will give you your life."* (15.27)
- P2: Ashwatthama, hand on the gem: *"This gem is worth more than all the wealth of the Kurus and the Pandavas."*
  (15.28)
- P3: *"Here is the gem. Here am I. But the grass will fall on the wombs of the Pandava women."* (15.31)
- P4: He turns the fire away from the men, toward the unborn. (15.33)

**p35. Krishna.** (10.16.1–8)
- P1: Krishna, calm: *"Uttara's child will be born dead. And he will live, a long life."* (16.8)
- P2: Ashwatthama: *"It won't happen."* (16.6–7)

**p36–37. The curse.** (10.16.9–12)
- p36 P1: Krishna: *"Every wise man will know you as a coward and a killer of children."* (16.9)
- p36 P2: *"For three thousand years you will wander this earth."* (16.10)
- p36 P3: *"No one will talk to you. You will have no place among people."* (16.10–11)
- p37 splash: *"You will smell of pus and blood. You will live in the wild, carrying every disease."* (16.12) The gem
  is gone from his forehead.

**p38. What was left.** (10.16.19–26)
- P1: He walks into the forest as they watch (16.19).
- P2: Bhima gives Draupadi the gem: *"The killer of your sons is beaten. Get up."* (16.25–26)
- P3: Box: *The gem that kept weapons, hunger and disease away from him stayed behind. He took the diseases with him.*

## Close (p39–41)

**p39.** Back to the cold open's forest. The figure walks on, smaller.
- Box: *Three thousand years, said Krishna.*
- Box: *The prayer says he never dies.*
- Box: *Some people say they have met him.*
- Last box (small): *Next: HANUMAN. The one who chose to stay.*

**p40. Facts used** (every verse cited above, grouped by page) and **What the texts don't say:**
- The Mahabharata gives the curse as 3,000 years (10.16.10). "He never dies" comes from the Chiranjeevi prayer, a
  later traditional verse that is not in the Mahabharata.
- The epic doesn't describe a bleeding wound on his forehead where the gem was; it says he would smell of pus and blood
  and carry every disease (10.16.12). The forehead wound is a later popular image.
- Sightings (Asirgarh fort, the Narmada's banks and elsewhere) are local legends, not scripture.
- Some retellings say Ashwatthama cut the gem out himself; the critical edition only says he gave it (10.16.19).
