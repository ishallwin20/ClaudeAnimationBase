# Navagraha Book 1: Surya, the Sun Who Shines for All

For ages 3–10. Pages are 14 × 22 cm (portrait), spreads 28 × 22 cm, 16 interior pages plus a 4-page cover. Art in the
Painted style (p5.brush). Text: 2–4 short lines a page, read-aloud rhythm, a few speech bubbles.

Morals (one per beat, never preached twice): help comes to those who ask with a true heart; be dependable, like the
sunrise; love makes room for others; keep learning, even when it is hard; when you are tired, turn to the light; Surya
shines on everyone, big or small.

## Pages

**Inside front cover.** "This book belongs to ____________" in a painted sun frame.

**p1 (title page).** SURYA · The Sun Who Shines for All · Navagraha Series, Book 1 · Rishi Katha
Art: a small smiling sun disc with lotus petals.

**p2–3. The world without light.** Art: a dark indigo sky; the asuras, small and gleeful, sit on the devas' clouds;
Aditi, seated on a hillside at left, palms joined, a tiny lamp before her. A faint gold glow is starting in the far
right sky.
- p2: Long, long ago, the asuras chased the devas out of the sky. / The world grew cold and dark.
- p3: Aditi, the mother of the devas, closed her eyes and prayed to the Light of the world. / And far away, the Light
  heard her.
- Bubble (Aditi): "Please, be born as my son, and help us!"

**p4–5. The egg they thought was lost.** Art: left, Kashyapa, worried, hands up; Aditi calm, holding a glowing golden
egg. Right, the egg bursts open: rays fill the page, a baby Surya in the light, the asuras running off the edge.
- p4: Soon Aditi held a tiny glowing egg. / She fasted and prayed for it, day after day.
- Bubble (Kashyapa): "Aditi! Won't all this fasting harm the egg?"
- p5: WHOOSH! Out burst a light brighter than a thousand lamps! / A voice from the sky named him Martanda. / The asuras
  could not bear his light, and ran away.

**p6–7. The chariot of the Sun.** Art: Surya in his golden one-wheeled chariot crossing a pink-and-gold dawn sky, left
to right, seven horses in a fan of colours, Aruna (the dawn) holding the reins, a village waking far below.
- p6: Every day, Surya rides a golden chariot with just one wheel. / Seven horses pull it across the sky.
- p7: Aruna, the dawn, holds the reins. / That's why the sky turns pink just before the Sun comes up!
- Bubble (Surya): "Good morning, everyone! Time to wake up!"

**p8–9. A little less shine.** Art: left, Sanjna shielding her eyes from Surya's glare. Right, Vishwakarma at his
workshop wheel, gently trimming Surya's glow; sparkling shavings fly up and turn into a spinning chakra and a trishul.
- p8: Surya married Sanjna, the daughter of Vishwakarma, the builder of the gods. / But Surya shone so brightly that
  Sanjna could hardly look at him.
- p9: So Surya let Vishwakarma trim away a little of his shine. / From the sparkly shavings, Vishwakarma made Vishnu's
  Sudarshan chakra and Shiva's trishul!
- Bubble (Surya): "For you, I will shine a little softer."

**p10–11. The fruit in the sky.** Art: left, Bal Hanuman leaping from a treetop toward a red rising sun, arms out.
Right, Indra on Airavata, the vajra flying; Hanuman tumbling, hand on his chin; then the gods around him, Surya holding
out a glowing gift.
- p10: One morning, baby Hanuman saw the red rising Sun. / "A big, juicy fruit!" he thought, and leapt into the sky.
- Bubble (Hanuman): "Yum!"
- p11: Indra's vajra bumped his chin, and down he tumbled. / Then the gods gave him gifts. Surya gave him a part of his
  own shine.
- Bubble (Surya): "And when you are ready, I will be your teacher."

**p12–13. Learning on the move.** Art: a long arc of sky from the eastern hills (left) to the western hills (right);
grown Hanuman flying backwards, facing Surya, listening, a palm-leaf book under his arm; Surya teaching as he rides.
- p12: When Hanuman grew up, he went to Surya to learn. / But Surya never stops, not even for a moment!
- p13: So Hanuman flew in front of his teacher, facing him, from the eastern hills to the western hills. / That is how
  he learned grammar, the rules of words. Learning takes effort, and Hanuman never gave up.

**p14–15. Turn to the light.** Art: a battlefield at dawn, soft and distant (no fighting shown); Rama, tired, leaning
on his bow; Sage Agastya beside him pointing to the rising sun. Right, Rama standing tall, palms joined, facing Surya,
glowing.
- p14: Long after, Rama stood on the battlefield, tired and worried. / Mighty Ravana was coming to fight once more.
- p15: Sage Agastya taught him a prayer to the Sun, the Aditya Hridayam. / Rama prayed, and his strength came back.
  When we feel tired, we can turn to the light.
- Bubble (Agastya): "Pray to the Sun, my child, and you will win."

**p16. Meet Surya.** Art: Surya smiling, two lotuses, the one-wheeled chariot small beside him.
- His day: Sunday, Ravivar (Ravi is another name for Surya).
- His colours: red and gold. He holds two lotuses.
- His family: mother Aditi, father Kashyapa. His children include Yama, Yamuna and Shani.
- Did you know? The Sun is a star! Its light takes about 8 minutes to reach us, and without it, nothing could grow.
- Be like Surya: shine on everyone, big or small.
- Next in the series: Chandra, the Moon.

**Inside back cover.** The Navagraha Series: the nine grahas in a ring (Surya, Chandra, Mangala, Budha, Brihaspati,
Shukra, Shani, Rahu, Ketu) with their weekdays; Rishi Katha logo; rishikatha.com.

**Back cover.** Blurb: "Why does the sky turn pink before sunrise? Who taught Hanuman? Meet Surya, the Sun who shines
for all, in this first book of the Navagraha Series." Rishi Katha logo, rishikatha.com, age 3–10.

## Facts used

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| The asuras had taken the three worlds from the devas; Aditi prayed to the Sun, who promised to be born as her son | Markandeya Purana, the Martanda story (Pargiter tr., cantos 101–105) |
| Aditi fasted while carrying the embryo; Kashyapa asked why she was "killing the egg" (māritam aṇḍam); the egg blazed and a voice from the sky named him Mārtaṇḍa; the asuras could not bear his light | Markandeya Purana, same cantos |
| The Sun's chariot has a single wheel and seven horses (the seven Vedic metres: Gayatri, Brihati, Ushnik, Jagati, Trishtubh, Anushtubh, Pankti) | Vishnu Purana 2.8 |
| Aruna, Vinata's elder son, became the Sun's charioteer; he is the red of dawn | Mahabharata, Adi Parva 16 (Astika Parva) |
| Sanjna, Vishwakarma's daughter, could not bear Surya's radiance; Vishwakarma placed him on his lathe and trimmed an eighth of it; from it he made Vishnu's discus, Shiva's trident and other weapons | Vishnu Purana 3.2; Markandeya Purana (same story) |
| Baby Hanuman leapt at the rising sun, taking it for a fruit; Indra's vajra struck his jaw (hanu) and he fell | Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 35 |
| The gods gave him boons; Surya gave him a hundredth part of his radiance and said he would teach him the shastras when he was able | Uttara Kanda 36.13–14 |
| Hanuman learned grammar facing the Sun, travelling with him from the eastern mountain to the western | Uttara Kanda 36.42 |
| Agastya came to the tired Rama before his fight with Ravana and taught him the Aditya Hridayam; Rama recited it and went on to defeat Ravana | Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 105 (Aditya Hridayam), 107–108 |
| Surya holds two lotuses; his children include Yama, Yamuna and Shani | Vishnu Purana 3.2; iconography per Vishnudharmottara / Brihat Samhita |
| Sunday is Ravivar, the day of Ravi (Surya) | the Hindu week (vāra) |

Left out on purpose: Chhaya, the shadow Sanjna left in her place (too complex for ages 3–6; it belongs in the Shani
book); Rahu at the sun in Uttara Kanda 35 (it belongs in the Rahu book).

Dialogue: speech bubbles are performance, not plot claims. Each says only what its source has the character say or do
(Aditi's prayer, Kashyapa's question, Surya's promise to teach, Agastya's advice); "Good morning, everyone!" and "Yum!"
claim no fact.
