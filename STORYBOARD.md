# Saanwaro nahi aayo (Mira's song), a follower collab

A poem in Mira's voice, written and sung by **Shivangi Singh (@thebrowngirl_ss)**, a follower. Style: **Miniature**
(new, Nov 2026; MINIATURE_GUIDE.md). 131.5 s, 9:16 (a 4 s harmonium and tanpura intro, then her recording; the table's
times are her recording's, +4 s in the video). Her reverb take (assets/vo/mira/shivangi_1_reverb.mp3, out of git)
carries the reel; the verse is written on the inscription band as she sings it, Devanagari with the English meaning.
Her recording sings verses 1, 2 and 4 (the "dhruv tara" verse is not sung): the reel follows the recording and the
caption carries the whole poem. Krishna himself never comes: the poem says he did not. He is seen only as his murti (the poet asked for it, 20 Nov: as a
little girl Mira sees Krishna's murti in a temple, is spellbound, and waits for him ever since; his crown stays on). No book card (collab): the card
is "follow both".

Not a retelling: no purana plot is claimed. Mira's iconography only (white odhni, ektara, Giridhar Gopal's murti,
dancing in ghungroo: "pag ghunghroo bandh Mira nachi re" is her own pad).

Sound (tools/mira_sfx.mjs): a tanpura on her Sa (~250 Hz, from her phrase endings), on its own stem, 12 dB under her sung
voice and ducked a little under it so it swells in her pauses; a harmonium (two reeds, Sa and Pa held) that opens the reel
for 4 s and answers each line in the pauses with a Kafi phrase, 5 dB under her voice; his flute under "Mohan roop" as he
shows her his form, fading as she grows up; the bansuri's last phrase on the card; her voice at 0.8; ektara
in the shrine; ghungroo on her steps; the temple bell; parrot wings; crickets and far thunder; the river, rain, the sea;
a chime as the lamp touches the water. One linear gain to -14 LUFS (no dynamic loudnorm).

Poet's notes (20 Nov), applied: line 2 पलकन छांवो उतारौ = to keep him under the shade of her eyelids (to see him): "I came
to keep him in the shade of my eyelids…"; line 3 मोहन = both Krishna's name and "the one who enchants", and बेहरूपी
because she was young and unaware of such charm (not a "player"): "A shape-shifter from a far-off land | showed me his
form: Mohan, the enchanter…"; the bahroopiya taking off Krishna's crown is gone (it could offend); the intro, levels
and garland as above. Still to confirm: line 11 (तोहरे मुख की लहरी देखे: "since I saw the play of light on your face").
