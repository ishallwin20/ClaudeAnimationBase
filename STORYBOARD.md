# Reel: "Krishna had 16,108 queens. Narad Muni went to CHECK." (Little Kanha, Part 3)

A ~57.6 s captioned Painted reel for followers (ends on a Follow card teasing Part 4). Narad Muni hears that Krishna,
in ONE body, lives with 16,000+ queens, each in her own palace, and flies to Dwarka to see how. In the first palace
Rukmini is fanning Krishna; Krishna leaps up, washes Narad's feet and puts the water on his own head. Narad tiptoes to
the next palace: Krishna is playing dice with his queen and Uddhava, and looks up: "Narad-ji! When did you ARRIVE?"
Palace after palace, Krishna is there, doing something else: playing with his kids, getting ready for his bath, offering
into the sacred fire, at sword practice, giving cows away, deep in meditation. Every palace, the same Krishna. Narad
laughs: "I see it now: your Yogamaya!" Krishna: "Don't be confused, my child. I live this way to teach the world."
And every queen believed he had never left HER palace.

```
Logline: a sage tries to work out how one man lives in 16,000 homes, and keeps walking in on him.
Why it should travel: a comedy montage where every gag is in the text; Narad's face gets more frazzled at every door;
         "When did you arrive?" is the running joke the Bhagavata itself tells; the multitasking hits every mom and
         every working person ("I need this power"). Follows on from the ad-22 Narad audience.
Hook:    Frame 0: Narad Muni close, one eyebrow up, a hand on his chin, Dwarka's gold palaces behind, caption up:
         "Krishna had 16,108 QUEENS," / "a palace for EACH." / "Narad Muni went to CHECK!" (gold)
World:   Dwarka on the sea at morning (rows of gold palaces) → the palace rooms of 10.69.9–12: coral pillars,
         sapphire walls, pearl festoons, jewelled lamps; each palace a different wall colour → a wall of windows →
         four windows, four queens → Dwarka at golden evening → the peach card.
Motif:   the doorway: Narad's head pokes in from the left, each time more frazzled (curious → shocked → sweating →
         dizzy → laughing).
Arc:     Narad: suspicious → touched (his feet washed) → baffled → frazzled → dizzy → laughs, understands → happy,
         singing. Krishna: calm and kind every time, always pleased to see him.
Ending rhyme: frame 0, Narad looks at us, suspicious → the end, he looks back at us over his shoulder and winks.
Comment lever (not a name call-out): "If YOU could be in TWO places at once, where would you GO? Comment!"
```

## Facts used (Srimad Bhagavatam, Skandha 10, unless noted)
- Krishna married the princesses Narakasura had imprisoned, each in her own palace, at the same time, in as many forms
  (10.59.33, 10.59.42). Narada wondered that one person in one body could marry 16,000 women in separate homes, and went
  to Dwarka to see (10.69.1–6).
- **The count:** the Bhagavata says 16,000 (10.59.33, 10.69.2, 10.69.7); the Vishnu Purana (5.31) says 16,100; with
  the eight chief queens (Rukmini and the others, 10.58, 10.83) that is the widely cited **16,108**, used in the hook.
- Dwarka's palaces: coral pillars, vaidurya, sapphire walls, pearl festoons, jewelled lamps (10.69.9–12).
- Palace #1: Rukmini herself fans Krishna with a chamara though a thousand maids attend her (10.69.13); Krishna rises,
  bows at Narada's feet, washes them and puts the water on his own head (10.69.14–15).
- Palace #2: Narada enters another queen's palace and finds Krishna playing dice with his beloved and Uddhava; Krishna
  honours him and asks, "When did you arrive?" (10.69.19–22).
- The montage, each in another palace (10.69.23–36): fondling his small children (23), preparing to bathe (23),
  offering oblations into the sacred fires (24), sword and shield practice (25), giving decorated cows to brahmanas
  (27), meditating on the Supreme (29).
- In every palace Narada saw the Lord in the same form (10.69.41).
- Narada laughed and spoke of the Lord's yogamaya (10.69.37–38); Krishna: "I observe religious principles to teach
  them to the world, my child, so do not be disturbed" (10.69.40). Narada departed, singing and remembering him
  (10.69.39, 10.69.44).
- Each queen saw that Krishna never left her palace and thought herself his favourite (10.61.2).
- Performance only, no plot claim: Narad's frazzled faces and peeking, the palace numbers on screen (the text doesn't
  number them), the wink.
