# The gods were TERRIFIED of one giant's wish. So a goddess hijacked his TONGUE. (reel/kumbhakarna-sleep)

A Cinematic reel (see CINEMATIC_GUIDE.md), 82 s, 9:16, with a voiceover: the narrator from the Ganga and Kailasa reels
(Sarvam `girish_en_devotional`, pace 1.2) and Kumbhakarna's two lines in `rustom_en_suspense` with the asura effect, a
synthesized SFX track and a Lyria score ducked under the voice. It ends on a follow card with no offer. A "known yet
unknown" explainer: everyone knows Kumbhakarna sleeps; few know the gods rigged his wish, or what it took to wake him.

```
Logline: Kumbhakarna, Ravana's brother, ate thousands of creatures as a newborn, ate apsaras and Indra's followers in
         heaven's garden, and when Indra struck him with the vajra he tore a tusk out of Airavata and stabbed Indra with
         it. After ten thousand years of tapas Brahma came to grant his wish, and the terrified gods begged: not him; give
         him delusion instead. Brahma sent Saraswati, goddess of speech, into his mouth, and he asked to sleep for many
         years. "So be it." She left, and he froze: what came out of my mouth? Brahma later eased it: six months asleep,
         one day awake. To wake him for the war they piled a mountain of meat, blew conches till the birds fell from the
         sky, and ran a thousand elephants over him. He woke up yawning, and hungry.
Why it should travel: the winning shape (an impossible force, then the twist: "So a goddess hijacked his TONGUE"); a
         famous name with an unknown cause; a comic spectacle at the end; and a tag-a-friend ask that spreads the reel.
Comment: "Who's the KUMBHAKARNA in YOUR house? Tag them all!"
Pinned:  "Tag the one who can sleep through anything 😴👇"
```

## Facts used (every plot beat, with its source)
- Kumbhakarna is Ravana's brother: Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 9–10.
- Soon after his birth, hungry, he ate thousands of living creatures; the frightened people went to Indra: Yuddha Kanda
  61.13–14.
- Indra struck him with the vajra; Kumbhakarna pulled out one of Airavata's tusks and struck Indra in the chest with it:
  Yuddha Kanda 61.15–17. (The reel's "Airavat" is the same elephant, spelled as most viewers say it.)
- He devoured seven apsaras in Nandana (Indra's garden), ten of Mahendra's followers, and sages and men: Uttara Kanda
  10.33 ("nandane 'psarasaḥ sapta mahendrānucarā daśa / anena bhakṣitā brahmanṛṣayo mānuṣās tathā").
- His tapas: ten thousand years; in summer amid the five fires, in the rains seated in the open, in winter in water:
  Uttara Kanda 10.3–5.
- When Brahma was about to grant his boon, the gods begged him not to, and asked that he be given delusion in the guise
  of a boon ("varavyājena moho 'smai dīyatām"): Uttara Kanda 10.31–34.
- Brahma called Saraswati and told her to become the rakshasa's speech; she entered his mouth: Uttara Kanda 10.35–38.
- He said: "To sleep for many years, O god of gods, is my wish" ("svaptuṃ varṣāṇy anekāni devadeva mamepsitam"); Brahma
  said "So be it" ("evam astu"): Uttara Kanda 10.39–40.
- When she left him he grieved, thinking: what words have come out of my mouth today? I was deluded by the gods:
  Uttara Kanda 10.41–42.
- Brahma, at Ravana's appeal, set it at six months asleep and one day awake: Yuddha Kanda 61.24–28. (Valmiki tells the
  sleep twice: as the boon Saraswati twisted, in Uttara 10, and as Brahma's curse, in Yuddha 61, where Ravana's appeal
  sets the six months. The reel takes the cause from Uttara 10 and the six months from Yuddha 61, and says only that
  "Brahma later eased it".)
- To wake him for the war: a heap of venison as high as Meru and buffaloes, pigs and blood set before him; conches,
  drums and gongs, clapping and roars so loud that birds fell from the sky; animals driven over him, logs and pillars;
  finally a thousand elephants ran up and down on his body, and he woke, yawning, hungry: Yuddha Kanda 60.30–55.

Not claimed: the popular "he meant to say Indrasana (Indra's throne) but said Nidrasana (a bed)" slip, a later
retelling not in Valmiki (and the Puranic Encyclopaedia's "nirdevatvam / nidravatvam" pun, from a later text). The
reel never says what he meant to ask for. The eating is shown without gore (a shadow falling, birds fleeing).

## Sound

and growl on the name; a roar and bursting wings; garden chimes, then a drone and a laugh; thunder, a hit and a sub on
the vajra; the tusk's crunch and Airavata's trumpet, a hit on the stab; fire, then a splash and icy wind; a conch for
Brahma; veena-like plucks and a shimmer for Saraswati, a whoosh into his mouth; a hit on SLEEP, a temple bell on "So be
it"; a whoosh out, a click as he freezes, a stinger (the score drops away under this); drones and snores through the
six months, a chime and birds on "ONE day"; marching drums and food thudding down; three conches and drums, birds
falling; elephants trumpeting and the stampede; a huge low groan for the yawn, a hit and a roar on HUNGRY; snoring in
the cave; chimes on the card. Score: Lyria take 1 of `tools/kumbha_score.txt` (90 BPM, 31 bars, 82 s brief; 84.1 s
back), ducked under the voice; loudnorm −14 LUFS.
