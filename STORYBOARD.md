# "Kerala was pulled out of the SEA" (ad 62 · Books 1–3 · 3D, the film look · Kerala Piravi, 1 Nov)

A 63.1 s legend + science reel in the **3D** style for Kerala Piravi (1 November). It is a standalone that teases the
Parshurama series. English VO (Sarvam `girish_en_devotional`, pace 1.3, `tools/kerala_vo.json`) with karaoke captions
over a synthesized SFX track (`assets/kerala_mix.wav`, -14 LUFS); music is added on Instagram. Shot times follow the
voice (`kerala_tl.js`), so the t column below is approximate. Parshurama appears as a lit silhouette on a sunset headland, so the Painted character stays the series'
look. The map is the real one: India's DEM, with Kerala in sharper detail (`src/data/kerala_dem.js`, 0.006°). The old
sea reaches the foot of the Ghats and draws back behind the flying axe from Gokarna to Kanyakumari. Shape: the Purana's
story, then science ("The legend says the sea drew back. The land says so too."), then a district call-out for comments.
Card: Books 1–3, set of 3 for ₹500, with "Parshurama series · coming soon".

**Framing (owner):** no casteist line. He fought arrogant kings and gave the earth away. The later part of the
Keralolpathi (settling families on the land) is left out.

## Facts used
| claim in the reel | source |
|---|---|
| Parshurama had won the earth from (arrogant) kings and gave all of it away, to Kashyapa | Mahabharata, Shanti Parva 12.49 (Ganguli, section XLIX): after his campaigns against the kings, he gives the earth to Kashyapa at the end of a horse sacrifice |
| Kashyapa told him he could not live on the land he had given away (he had to leave it) | MBh 12.49: Kashyapa asks him to leave his (Kashyapa's) earth and go to the southern ocean's shore |
| The ocean made new land for him | MBh 12.49.66: the ocean withdraws and creates for Jamadagni's son the land of Shurparaka on the western shore (*aparānta*) ([Wikipedia, Shurparaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shurparaka), citing MBh 12:49; Purana Encyclopaedia s.v. Śūrpāraka: "the land formed by the withdrawal of the ocean"). The reel says only "new land for him", not that the MBh names Kerala. It does not: scholars trace the legend's move from Konkan to Kerala (C. Vielle, *The regionalisation of the itihāsa-purāṇa tradition: the case of Paraśurāma's land creation, from Konkan to Kerala*, UCLouvain) |
| Kerala's own legend: at Gokarna he prayed to Varuna and threw his axe; the sea drew back as far as it flew, Gokarna to Kanyakumari | The Keralolpathi (a Malayalam chronicle, 17th–19th c.), as summarised by the University of Calicut (SDE course HIS1C02: "Parasurama threw his parasu or axe across the sea from Gokarnam to Kanyakumari… and water receded up to the spot where it fell"). Varuna and Bhumi blessing him at Gokarna: encyclopedic retellings of the same legend. The other version (thrown from Kanyakumari to Gokarna) is not used. Captions label it "Kerala's own legend" |
| Kerala is called Parashurama Kshetram (Bhargava Kshetram) | Same Calicut module: "the land… constituted the land of Kerala, otherwise called Bhargavakshetram or Parasuramakshetram" |
| Kerala's coastal plain is young land; thousands of years ago the sea came further inland | Holocene geology of the Kerala coast: transgression and regression phases, a stabilised mid-Holocene sea level followed by its fall, shorelines prograding seaward (beach ridges, spits, barrier islands); e.g. Padmalal et al. (Gondwana Geol. Soc.) and INQUA abstracts on the Kerala coast. No date is given on screen ("thousands of years ago") |
| The backwaters began as a bay of the sea, shut in by sand bars | "Vembanad Lake evolved from a sea embayment through the progressive growth of a sand bar"; Kuttanad is the silted-up part of the old Vembanad lagoon (Padmalal et al.) |
| Kuttanad is farmed below sea level | Kuttanad is the lowest region in India, farmed about 1–2 m below mean sea level (its below-sea-level farming system is a FAO GIAHS site, 2013) |
| Kerala Piravi, 1 November | Kerala state formed 1 November 1956 (States Reorganisation Act) |
| The 14 districts and where they are | Kerala's districts, at their headquarters (approximate) |

The reel makes no claim that science "proves" the legend. It only puts the two side by side ("the land says so too": the
shoreline did move out).

## Build
- `src/scenes/kerala_tl.js` (captions and timeline), `kerala_props.js` (the silhouette, the axe, the 2D shore, the
  Kerala DEM patch, the coastline, the old sea), `kerala.js` (shots), `kerala_cover.js` + `cover.html`.
- `src/data/kerala_dem.js`: `node tools/terrain.mjs --name=kerala --bbox=73.6,7.9,78.2,15.0 --z=9 --res=.006`.
- Voice: `node tools/sarvam_tts.mjs --script=tools/kerala_vo.json` → `assets/kerala_vo.wav` + `src/scenes/kerala_vo.js`.
- `tools/kerala_sfx.mjs` → `assets/kerala_sfx.wav`, then the mix with the voice → `assets/kerala_mix.wav` (-14 LUFS).
- Render: `node render.mjs --clip --out=out/kerala_out/kerala.mp4`; cover: `node render.mjs --page=cover.html --stills=0
  --out=out/kerala_out` (rename to cover.png).
