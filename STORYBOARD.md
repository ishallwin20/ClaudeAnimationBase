# "HANUMAN is still alive. This is what he does ALL DAY." (ad 99 · Hanuman series Part 10, the finale · Painted)

A 77.4 s reel in the **Painted** style, Bangers captions, retro SFX, no voice; the no-price Navadurga card. The last
part of the Hanuman series (HANUMAN_SERIES.md), built to be the most shareable of the ten: the shortest, the cutest,
and the one that ends in the viewer's own room.

**The idea:** the plan's hook was "Where is HANUMAN right NOW?". The texts give two addresses (a plantain grove on
Gandhamadana in the Mahabharata, Kimpurusha-varsha in the Bhagavata), so "where" has no single answer. What all three
texts agree on is what he is *doing*: listening to Ram's story. That is the hook ("this is what he does all day"), the
honest middle ("two books, two addresses, one answer") and the adorable centre: the strongest being alive, sitting like
a child at story time, cheering his own leap and checking his own tail when the song reaches Lanka. Then his own verse
from the Bhagavata (forest monkeys, and Ram still made us his friends), and the turn to the viewer: an old prayer says
he is wherever the story is told, and you just heard ten parts of it.

**Motif:** the song. Gold notes drift in from off screen in the hook, come down with the singers in the grove, show the
story in a gold-rimmed cloud, and end as a music box in a child's bedroom. The hand cupped to his ear opens the reel,
returns in every "listening" shot and closes it in the ask.

## Sound (tools/hanu10_sfx.mjs → assets/hanu10_sfx.wav → assets/hanu10_mix.wav, ffmpeg loudnorm, about −16.5 LUFS)
One thread: the gandharvas' song, a plucked veena phrase in a major pentatonic over a soft tanpura. It drifts in from
the right in the hook, is sung in the grove and in Kimpurusha-varsha, slows under Ram's embrace, **stops** when he
stands (an "ahem"), gives way to his own five sung notes, and returns as a music box in the child's room and under the
ask. Round it, the series' retro foley: a rising tone and a chime as Ram goes; grove birds, a snore and its whistle,
Bhima's steps and gasp, a boing as one eye opens; pops for the two pictures; a whoosh for the leap and his own cheer;
crackle, a gasp and three puffs for the tail; two heartbeats under the embrace; a big chime and giggles for the hug;
four rising pops for the row; crickets and dadi's murmur; a boing as he pops up behind the phone; ten rising plinks for
the ten parts.

## Facts used
Read on 16 Nov 2026. My own reading of the Sanskrit where the edition is Sanskrit.

**Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda** (IIT Kanpur edition, heritage.iitk.ac.in/VRAM, verse by verse):
- 40.16: "as long as Ram's story moves over the earth, so long will my breath stay in my body" (his own request;
  Part 8 told the scene in full). 40.20: Ram: "so it will be."
- 40.17–18: "and let apsaras make me hear this divine story of yours; hearing the nectar of your deeds I will drive
  away that longing (utkaṇṭhā), as the wind a streak of cloud." The reel's "when I miss you, your story will blow my
  longing away" is this.
- 108.33–36: as he leaves the world Ram tells Hanuman: "you set your mind on living, do not break your promise; as
  long as my stories go round in the world, be glad"; Hanuman: "as long as your story moves in the world I will stay
  on earth."

**Mahabharata, Vana Parva** (BORI critical edition, GRETIL; chapter.verse numbers are its own):
- 3.146.42: on Gandhamadana's slopes, a lovely plantain grove many yojanas wide. 146.59–60: Hanuman, asleep in the
  middle of the plantain grove, yawns. 146.64: Bhima sees him in the grove on a broad stone slab. 146.73: at Bhima's
  roar he "opened his eyes a little". 146.70: the plantains are golden.
- 3.147.37: "when Ram was established I asked him: as long as Ram's story lasts in the worlds, so long may I live. So
  be it, he said." 147.38: Ram ruled for eleven thousand years, then went to heaven. 147.39: "so here apsarases and
  gandharvas always delight me, singing that hero's deeds."
- "An age later": Ram's story is of the Treta age, the Pandavas' of the Dvapara; the reel gives no number of years.
- "Himalayan": the Pandavas reach Gandhamadana from Badari, in the Himalaya (Vana 140–145).

**Bhagavata Purana** (vedabase.io, Sanskrit and translation):
- 5.19.1: in Kimpurusha-varsha the great devotee Hanuman, with the people of that land, worships Ram, the elder
  brother of Lakshman, Sita's beloved, with unbroken devotion.
- 5.19.2: with Arshtishena he listens intently as gandharvas sing his lord's most auspicious story, and he himself
  sings this.
- 5.19.7: "not birth in a great family, not good looks, not speech, not wit, not form is the cause of his pleasure:
  for even us forest-dwellers, who have none of these, Lakshman's elder brother made his friends." On screen: "Not
  birth. Not beauty. Not brains. We were only forest monkeys. And Ram still made us his friends."
- 5.19.8: "god or demon, monkey or man: let each worship Ram with his whole self." On screen: "let everyone love Ram."
- 5.16.9: Kimpurusha-varsha lies beyond the Himalaya (the three ranges Nishadha, Hemakuta and Himalaya bound
  Hari-varsha, Kimpurusha-varsha and Bharata-varsha): the reason the set shows the snow range behind the meadow.

**A traditional verse, quoted as a prayer and not as scripture:** "yatra yatra raghunātha-kīrtanaṁ tatra tatra
kṛta-mastakāñjalim / bāṣpa-vāri-paripūrṇa-locanaṁ mārutiṁ namata rākṣasāntakam": wherever Ram's praise is sung, there
is Maruti, hands joined at his head, eyes brimming with tears. It is recited everywhere before Ramayan readings; I
could not trace it to a source text, so the caption says "an old prayer says", the storyboard says so here, and the
pinned comment names it as traditional.

Not claimed: one address for him (the reel says the two books give two); that Bhima asked what he does there (Bhima
asks who he is; Hanuman tells his own story and ends with 147.37–39); how the Kimpurushas or Arshtishena look (not
drawn: only gandharvas, as 5.19.2 has them); that he lives *because* of anyone's telling (the texts say "as long as");
that he is seen where the story is told (the prayer says he is there; the child does not see him); the vulgate's line
that Sita's grace brings him heavenly food there (3.147.37f*, starred in the critical edition: left out, though it is
adorable); a count of years.
Performance only (no plot claims): his hand cupped to his ear; the bananas in his lap, the snore bubble; the singers
coming down on clouds; the story shown in a cloud, and what it shows him of (the leap, the burning tail, the embrace:
all told in earlier reels from Valmiki); his cheering, blowing on his tail and reaching up; the "ahem"; the two small
vanaras and their head-scratching; Varuna standing for "god" and Vibhishana for "demon" in the row; dadi, the boy, the
phone; the heart chips.
Corrections to the series plan: the hook changed from "Where is HANUMAN right NOW?" (see The idea); the Bhima scene is
Vana 146–147, not 148–150; the ask changed from "is there a Hanuman temple near you? comment your city" to "comment JAI
SHRI RAM" (it is what the last beat sets up: he is listening), with the city ask moved to the pinned comment.
