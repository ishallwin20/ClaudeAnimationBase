# Ad 51 — "Tonight at MIDNIGHT, Maa Lakshmi asks ONE question." (Sharad Purnima / Kojagari · Books 1–3)

A 46.6 s captioned Painted reel for Sharad Purnima (25 Oct 2026), the night also called Kojagari ("ko jāgarti?", who
is awake?). No voiceover, Bangers captions, SFX only (music added on Instagram), the Books 1–3 ₹500 card. Built and
posted the same day: it is about *tonight*, and it ends on the viewer, who is awake because they're watching it.

```
Logline: On Sharad Purnima the moon is full and kheer sits in the moonlight. At midnight Maa Lakshmi comes down asking
         "KO JAGARTI?" (who is awake?). Her owl checks the windows: everyone is asleep, except one boy on his phone
         under the razai. He sees her, joins his hands, "JAI MAA LAKSHMI!", and the coins pour in. Then she turns to
         us: you're watching this, so YOU are awake. Answer her: comment JAI MAA LAKSHMI.
Cover:   cover.html (src/scenes/kojagari_cover.js): Lakshmi before the full moon and its 16 kalas, the owl waving;
         "TONIGHT AT MIDNIGHT / Maa Lakshmi asks ONE question", "को जागर्ति? / WHO IS AWAKE?".
Why it should travel: posted on the night itself; the question is put to the viewer, whose being awake and watching
         IS the answer; the comment ask is a prayer (JAI MAA LAKSHMI), which is also what the text asks of whoever is
         awake. Share lever: "send it to the friend who's already asleep".
Hook:    Frame 0: one subject, the full moon on indigo; "Tonight at MIDNIGHT, / Maa Lakshmi asks / ONE question." up.
World:   a moonlit town of flat roofs: indigo sky, a silver full moon, lime-washed blue walls, diyas on every parapet
         (the text has lamps lit after moonrise), brass bowls of kheer. Colour arc: cool silver-blue night → Lakshmi's
         gold light into the boy's room → gold coins → the brand peach card.
Motifs:  THE QUESTION (asked on the roof → answered in the window → put to the viewer). THE KHEER (on the parapet, on
         every sill). THE OWL'S HEAD (spins round from the sleepers; spins round to us at "YOU").
Ending rhyme: frame 0, the moon and a question → Lakshmi looking straight at us, the question turned on the viewer.
Arcs:    Lakshmi: serene (descends, eyes closed) → calling out → amused at the boy → eyes closed with him in prayer →
                  giving → turns to us, a wink
         Owl: inspector (back of his head to us) → deadpan "nope" at the sleepers → "FOUND ONE!" (points) → dazzled by
              her light: shades → flies to her → head spin to us
         Aarav: grinning at his phone under the razai → notices → GASP (drops the phone) → hands joined → starry-eyed
```

## Facts used
| claim in the reel | source |
|---|---|
| On Kojagara (Ashvin Purnima) night: lamps lit after moonrise, milk pudding (kheer) kept "in many vessels in the bright moonlight", a night vigil | Nārada Purāṇa 124.47–52 (English translation on Wisdom Library) |
| At midnight Mahalakshmi moves over the earth asking "who is awake?" and gives wealth to the one who is awake (and worships her) | Nārada Purāṇa 124.53–55; the verse *niśīthe varadā lakṣmīḥ ko jāgartīti bhāṣiṇī…*, attributed to the Liṅga Purāṇa in the ritual digests (Tithitattva, Nirṇaya Sindhu) |
| "को जागर्ति?" / "KO JAGARTI?" = "Who is awake?" | the same verse (*ko jāgarti*) |
| The blessing goes to the boy only after he joins his hands and prays | the text: awake *and* worshipping her |

Worded as tradition, not scripture: "the moon is **said to** shine with all 16 kalas" (a popular belief; no primary
passage verified). Not claimed: that moonlight turns the kheer into amṛta; the dice-playing of the vigil (in the verse,
left out of the reel). Performance only: the owl as her scout, his head spins, the shades, the family under one razai
and their snores, the phone under the blanket, the boy's gasp, the wink.
