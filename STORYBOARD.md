# Ad 57: "BHIMA couldn't lift an old monkey's TAIL" (Painterly)

A Books 1–3 ad in the **Painterly** style (PAINTERLY_GUIDE.md): a painted banana grove on Gandhamadana, Bhima and a
sleepy monkey across his path, told in dialogue captions with shot/reverse close-ups. No voiceover; a mild Lyria score
(bansuri, tanpura, soft strings) under natural, slightly retro SFX. ~80 s, ends on the Books 1–3 ₹500 card.

## Facts used (Mahabharata, Vana Parva, Tirtha-yatra Parva; Ganguli sections CXLV–CLI = Critical Edition 3.146–3.150)

| beat | source |
|---|---|
| A wind from the north-east brings a celestial thousand-petalled lotus, "effulgent as the sun"; Draupadi asks Bhima to fetch more | CXLV (opening) |
| Bhima goes up the Gandhamadana, roaring, smashing trees and plantain (banana) trunks; animals and birds flee | CXLV |
| Hanuman, knowing Bhima is his brother, lies across the narrow path in the plantain grove, drowsy, yawning, lashing his tail | CXLV |
| "Ill as I am, I was sleeping sweetly. Why hast thou awakened me?" | CXLV |
| Bhima: "Arise! give me passage"; Hanuman: "I have no strength to rise; I am suffering from illness. If go thou must, go by overleaping me" | CXLVI |
| Bhima: the Supreme Soul pervades every body, so he will not leap over him; else he would leap "even as Hanuman had bounded over the ocean" | CXLVI |
| Hanuman: "Who is that Hanuman?" Bhima: "He is even my brother … I am equal unto him in energy, strength and prowess" | CXLVI |
| Hanuman: "In consequence of age, I have no strength to get up. … go, moving aside my tail" | CXLVI |
| Bhima takes the tail with his left hand, slightingly; it won't move; then with both arms; brows contracted, eyes rolling, face wrinkled, covered in sweat | CXLVI |
| Bhima stands bashful, bows with joined hands, asks forgiveness and who he is; "I am a monkey, by name Hanuman", begotten by the wind-god | CXLVI |
| Bhima: "now have I seen my elder brother"; asks to see the form in which he bounded over the ocean | CXLVIII |
| Hanuman grows "to the height reached by the Vindhya", like a golden mountain; Bhima closes his eyes | CXLIX |
| He contracts his body and embraces Bhima; Bhima's fatigue goes; Hanuman with tears in his eyes | CL |
| "When thou shalt give leonine roars … remaining on the flagstaff of Arjuna's car will I emit fierce shouts" | CL |

Both are sons of Vayu (Bhima: "the offspring of the wind-god", CXLVI; Hanuman: "begotten by the wind-god", CXLVI).
Bhima carries a gold-backed bow and arrows on this journey (CXLV), not his mace, so he carries the bow here.
Performance only (no plot claim): the faces, Hanuman's yawn and lazy eye while Bhima strains, the sweat drops, the
bird that lands on the tail.
