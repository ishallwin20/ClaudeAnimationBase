# "Diwali is FIVE days. The god of DEATH gets THREE." (ad 58 · Books 1–3 · 3D)

A 59.7 s explainer in the **3D** style, built to post before Dhanteras (6 Nov 2026; Diwali 8 Nov). It follows the
shape of ads 50 and 54: a practice every family does at the next festival, a number, sourced. On top of that it tells
a story. The five days of Dipavali come from one chapter of the Padma Purana. Three of them belong to Yama: a lamp put
outside the door for him on Dhanteras; water offered to him by his fourteen names on day two; and on day five he goes
to his sister Yamuna's house and she feeds him, which is why brothers eat from their sister's hand on Bhai Dooj.

One village carries all five days. Night one is the misty lane, where Death rides his buffalo up to the single diya,
halts at its light and turns back. Day two is a dawn ghat. On the new moon every roof and step is lit, and on day four
a hill across the river is ringed with lamps. Then a beat of silence, and the same rider, now gentle in the morning
light, walks to his sister's door. Inside, a thali and his crowned shadow on the wall.

English VO (Sarvam `girish_en_devotional`, pace 1.2). Karaoke captions are timed to the voice. The SFX run under it,
with a mild Lyria score (0.18, ducked under the voice, dropped out for the beat of silence). Card: Books 1–3, set of 3
for ₹500.

## Facts used
| claim in the reel | source |
|---|---|
| Diwali is five days; the reel's tag "Padma Purana" | Padma Purana, Uttara Khanda 122 ("The Celebration of Dīpāvalī", Motilal tr.; [wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365701.html)): the 13th and 14th of Kartika's dark half, the new moon, the 1st and the 2nd of the bright half. The Skanda Purana's Kartika Mahatmya gives the same days |
| Day one, Dhanteras: one lamp goes outside the door, for Yama… and untimely death stays away | Padma Uttara 122 (v. 4–20): "on the thirteenth day of the dark half of Kārtika, a man should offer a light to Yama outside (his house). Thereby untimely death is avoided." Skanda Purana, quoted in the digests: *kārttikasyāsite pakṣe trayodaśyāṃ niśāmukhe / yamadīpaṃ bahir dadyād apamṛtyur vinaśyati* |
| "Families turn it to face SOUTH, Yama's direction" | Yama is the guardian of the south (the dikpala), and the tarpan to him is given facing south (Dharma Sindhu: *dakṣiṇāmukho dadyāt*). Pointing the Yama diya south is family practice, which is why the line says "families", not "the scriptures". The reel never says south is wrong on other nights |
| Day two, morning: a holy bath, then water for Yama by his 14 names (the names on screen) | Padma Uttara 122: "He who would bathe in the morning on Caturdaśī in Dīpāvalī does not see Yama's world"; the libations with the names "Yama, Dharmarāja, Mṛtyu, Antaka, Vaivasvata, Kāla, Sarvabhūtakṣaya, Audumbara, Dadhna, Nīla, Parameṣṭhin, Vṛkodara, Citra, Citragupta" (14). Dharma Sindhu has the same tarpan with sesame, facing south |
| Day three, the new moon, is Lakshmi's night | Padma Uttara 122 (v. 20–21): Lakshmi is woken on the new-moon night; "for one year Lakṣmī does not leave him" |
| Day four, Govardhan | Padma Uttara 122 (v. 32–42): on the first of the bright half, Govardhana is worshipped in the morning |
| Day five: the god of death goes to his sister's house; long ago, on this day, Yamuna fed her brother in her own home | Padma Uttara 122 (v. 89–103): "Formerly, on the second day of the bright half of Kārtika, Yama was fed and honoured by Yamunā in her house" |
| So on Bhai Dooj brothers eat from their sister's hand | Padma Uttara 122: "the wise should not eat at home (on this day). They should affectionately take a nourishing meal at their sister's hand; gifts should duly be given to sisters" |
| "The god of death gets three" | days one (the lamp), two (the bath and tarpan) and five (Yama Dvitiya, when Yama is worshipped in the morning), as above |

Checked and not used:
- The popular Dhanteras story of King Hima's son (Yama coming as a snake, dazzled by the lamps and the gold). It comes
  from the vrat katha, not the purana, so it stays out.
- "Never point a lamp south on other days". This is in the explainer sites, but we found no verse for it.
- "14 lamps on Choti Diwali". This is a common custom, but chapter 122 gives 14 *names*, not 14 lamps.
- The tilak on Bhai Dooj. This is a custom, not in chapter 122.

**Performance only (no claim):**
- Yama's look: the Cinematic rig in silhouette, on his buffalo.
- Him riding up the lane and turning back at the light. This shows "untimely death stays away" without saying how.
- The village, the ghat and the river.
- Lakshmi's rangoli footprints at the door, which are a household custom.
- The hill ringed with lamps.
- His shadow on the wall over the thali.
- The month naming. The Padma counts Kartika purnimanta, so its "dark half of Kartika" is Ashvin's in the amanta calendar.

"face its south is direction", so that line was reworded to "turn it to face south, Yama's direction".
