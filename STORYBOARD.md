# The tortoise who couldn't keep quiet (the first Fable reel: FABLE_GUIDE.md)

**Format** (from a viral reposted short the owner sent, 1 Nov 2026): a black 9:16 frame; a bold two-line headline in
white with one yellow keyword; a 3:2-ish picture band in the middle; no captions, no voice. The story is told only by
acting and by thought bubbles that hold grey pencil doodles. ~30 s, mostly locked camera, one set per act.

**Headline** (the whole time, frame 0 included): *This Is Why You Don't Need / To* **Reply** *To Everyone*

**Logline:** A tortoise wants to leave a dried-up pond with his two swan friends, so he thinks up a way to fly: he
bites the middle of a stick while they carry the ends, promising not to open his mouth; over a town the people below
laugh at "the flying cart wheel", and he opens his mouth to answer them.

**World:** warm, dusty, almost empty sets like the reference: a cracked pond bed under a pale ochre sky (act 1), then
open sky over a town of tiled roofs and a low mud wall (act 2). Muted palette: sand, ochre, sage, the swans' white,
one orange (their beaks). The light doesn't change; the tortoise's face does (sad, proud, cross, red).

**Motif:** the "zipped mouth" doodle. The swan shows it before take-off, again mid-flight, and once more after the fall.

## Cast
- **Kambugriva**, the tortoise (`tortoise()` in src/fable.js): olive shell, sage skin, big pale googly eyes with heavy lids.
- **Sankata and Vikata**, the two swans (`hamsa()` in src/fable.js: white hamsas with orange beaks); Sankata (the lead, right) has
  a little crest.
- Two town kids on Kartikeya's rig (`pal`, a girl and a boy) behind a mud wall.

## Facts used
- Panchatantra, Book 1 (Mitrabheda), "The Tortoise and the Swans": the tortoise Kambugriva and the swans Sankata and
  Vikata live at a lake; in a drought the swans decide to leave for another lake; the tortoise asks to come, and
  himself proposes the plan: he will grip the middle of a stick in his mouth while they carry the ends; the swans
  warn him not to speak on the way. Over a town the people look up and say the birds are carrying something like a
  cart wheel; the tortoise opens his mouth to answer ("what is this uproar?"), loses the stick, falls and is killed.
  Translations: A. W. Ryder (1925), F. Edgerton (1924). The same tale is Jataka 215 (Kacchapa Jataka) and the
  Hitopadesha (Book 4).
- The fall is shown only as a drop out of frame and an off-screen thud. The kids, the doodles' content beyond the lake,
  the stick plan, the warning and the cart wheel, and every reaction are performance, not plot claims.
