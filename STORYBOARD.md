# "The moment HANUMAN left, ARJUNA's chariot turned to ASH." (ad 98 · Hanuman series Part 9 · Painted)

A 95.8 s reel in the **Painted** style, Bangers captions, retro SFX, no voice; the no-price Navadurga card. Part 9 of
the Hanuman series (HANUMAN_SERIES.md): one of the two "power" episodes, shot as cinema: a build-up through the war, a
held silence when he leaves the banner, then the blaze.

**The idea:** everyone knows the ape on Arjuna's flag; few know how he got there or what happened the moment he left.
The reel follows one promise: made to Bhima in a plantain grove, kept through eighteen days (he roars, he even leaps at
Karna's banner), and ended the instant Krishna steps off the chariot. Then the reveal in Krishna's own words: the
chariot had burned long before and stood only because he sat on it.

**What the text says, and how the reel keeps to it:** in Shalya 61 the order is Krishna down (61.11), the ape vanishes
(61.12), the chariot blazes and falls as ash (61.13–14); the reason Krishna gives is that *he* sat on it (61.18). The
hook's sentence is true as a sequence, and the reel never says Hanuman held the chariot together: the caption at the
vanish is "The moment Krishna stepped off, Hanuman vanished from the flag", and the closing line gives each his own
part ("Krishna held it together. And Hanuman kept his promise till the last moment").

## Facts used
**Mahabharata**, read on 16 Nov 2026 in the BORI critical edition (Sanskrit, GRETIL e-text; chapter.verse numbers are
its own) beside K. M. Ganguli's translation (Project Gutenberg), which follows the vulgate: his Vana CL = critical
3.150, his Shalya 62 = critical 9.61. My own reading of the Sanskrit.
- Vana 3.146–147 (the recap; ad 57 told it in full): Bhima cannot move the old monkey's tail; it is Hanuman, his
  elder brother, both sons of Vayu.
- 3.150.1–3: Hanuman draws in his great form and embraces Bhima; Bhima's tiredness goes; Hanuman speaks "with tears
  in his eyes, his voice choked".
- 3.150.7: "let your seeing me not be fruitless: as a brother, choose a boon." 3.150.8–9: "if you wish, I will go to
  Hastinapura and kill the petty sons of Dhritarashtra… or grind that city with a rock."
- 3.150.11–12: Bhima: "all this is as good as done by you… with you as our protector, by your power we will defeat
  all our enemies."
- 3.150.13–15: "out of brotherhood and affection I will do what pleases you: when you plunge into the enemy army and
  roar like a lion, I will swell your roar with my own; seated on Arjuna's banner I will let out terrible roars that
  take your enemies' lives." Saying this, he vanished.
- Bhishma 6.23.20 (= Bhagavad Gita 1.20): Arjuna is *kapi-dhvaja*, "he whose banner bears the ape".
- Karna 8.37.7–9: Susharma pierces Arjuna's banner with an arrow; "that great ape, Vishvakarma's work, roared a great
  roar, terrifying"; hearing it the army "stood motionless in great fear" (37.10 likens it to a still flowering wood).
- Karna 8.63.65–70: as Arjuna and Karna close, Karna's banner is the *hasti-kakshya* (an elephant's girth rope,
  jewelled); Arjuna's ape, "jaws wide, terrible", "wishing to fight, leapt and struck the rope with nails and teeth, as
  Garuda a snake"; the rope, with its little bells, rushed at the ape; the two banners fought first.
- Shalya 9.61.1–7: evening after Duryodhana's fall (the eighteenth day); the Pandavas reach the camp and get down from
  their chariots.
- 9.61.8–10: Krishna to Arjuna: "take down the Gandiva and the two inexhaustible quivers; then I will get down after
  you. Get down yourself: this is for your good." Arjuna does so.
- 9.61.11: then Krishna lets go of the horses' reins and gets down. 9.61.12: when the lord of beings had got down,
  the divine ape, the banner of Arjuna, vanished.
- 9.61.13–14: the great chariot, burnt (before) by Drona and Karna with divine weapons, blazed up quickly with fire
  and fell to the ground as ash, with its quivers, reins, horses and yoke.
- 9.61.15–17: the Pandavas are amazed; Arjuna, hands joined, bowing: "Govinda, why has this chariot been burnt by
  fire? what is this great wonder?"
- 9.61.18–19: "It was burnt before by many kinds of weapons, Arjuna; because I sat on it, it did not fall apart in
  battle. Now, burnt by the brahmastra's energy, it has fallen apart, released by me, now that your work is done."
  9.61.27: Yudhishthira names "the brahmastra released by Drona and Karna".

Not claimed: that Hanuman's presence held the chariot together (the text credits Krishna's sitting on it; the popular
telling often adds Hanuman, the reel does not); that Hanuman fought or struck anyone in the war beyond the roar and the
banner fight; that the fire had "no visible source" (Ganguli's vulgate reading; the critical text says it blazed with
fire); why Krishna asked Arjuna to get down first beyond "it is for your good" (the usual explanation, that Arjuna
would have burned, is an inference and is left to the viewer); that the ape first came onto the banner because of this
promise (Adi 216 already describes a divine ape on the banner of the chariot Agni gave; the reel only shows the
promise and then Hanuman on the flag); how many years lay between the grove and the war ("years before" only).
Kept out of frame: 9.61.14 burns the horses with the chariot. The camp shots frame the car with the yoke pole running
off screen, so the reel neither shows that nor shows them spared.
Corrections to the series plan: "break your enemies' spirit" is "roars that take your enemies' lives" (softened on
screen to "I will roar with you"); "burns to ash with no fire in sight" is dropped (see above); Vana 150 is the
critical edition's number too, but Shalya 62 (Ganguli) is 9.61 there; two war scenes the plan did not have were added
from Karna Parva (the roar, the banner fight).
Performance only (no plot claims): Bhima's sweat and the monkey's yawn; the boulder in Hanuman's hand and the little
city in the bubble; Hanuman waving, yawning and bowing on the banner; the three foot soldiers; the flying bells; Drona
and Karna drawn on foot, side by side (they fought Arjuna on different days, from chariots: the shot is a summary of
61.13, not one moment); the blue glow in Krishna's bubble; Hanuman on a cloud at the end.
