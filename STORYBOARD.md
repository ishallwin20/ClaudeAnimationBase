A 48.8-second vertical reel (1080×1920, 24 fps, bpm 90) for Rishi Katha's Navadurga picture books, starring Shiva, Vasuki,
Mooshak and Parvati. Scene: [src/scenes/snake.js](src/scenes/snake.js), sets and props:
[src/scenes/snake_props.js](src/scenes/snake_props.js), characters: [src/shiva.js](src/shiva.js),
[src/parvati.js](src/parvati.js), [src/bappa.js](src/bappa.js) (Mooshak), brand images: `src/scenes/rk_images.js` (drawn with
`picture()`), sound effects: [tools/snake_sfx.mjs](tools/snake_sfx.mjs) → `assets/snake_sfx.wav`. No voiceover: captions carry it.

## Instagram caption

Why would a god wear a deadly snake around his neck? 🐍 The answer begins with poison.

When the devas and asuras churned the ocean of milk, Vasuki, king of serpents, was the rope. Out rose Halahala, a poison that could end the world. Everyone ran. Shiva drank it, Parvati held his throat, and it turned blue: Neelkanth. Many tellings say Shiva honoured Vasuki with the place right where the poison stopped.

To us, a snake means fear, death and time. Shiva doesn't run from any of them. He wears them.

Parvati once feared her own power too. How Shiva helped her master it, and how she became Maa Chandraghanta, the Fierce Protector, is Book 3 of our Navadurga picture-book series (ages 4–8). Get the set of 3 for ₹600 at rishikatha.com (link in bio).
