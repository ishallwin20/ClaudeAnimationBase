# Ramayan · Part 4: "The Crow and the Grass" (ad 36 · the Ramayan series)

Part 4 of the 12-part Ramayan series (format in ad 33's storyboard). Painted, **no voice** (owner, 23 Oct 2026, after
Part 3: "People love great use of SFX. Gives retro vibes. Loud BGM kills it"): Bangers captions carry the story, the
characters talk with moving mouths, and a retro cartoon SFX track sits under it in the manner of the Shumbha (ad 14) and
Bal Hanuman sun reels. The only music is a soft tanpura under the Lanka scene, as mild as the Sindoor reel's beds. 64.1 s,
levelled to about -17 LUFS. The voiced cut (70.1 s) is in git history and out/rp4_out. The hook: Ram once fired a Brahmastra at
a crow, with a blade of grass. The twist: Sita told it years later in Lanka, as a secret only Ram would know, and asked
why he spared the demon who took her.

## Facts used (Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda 38; valmikiramayan.net numbering)
| claim in the reel | source |
|---|---|
| At Chitrakoot, near the hermitage, by the water, Ram and Sita rest; they fall asleep | Sundara 38.12–14 ([sarga 38](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/sundara/sarga38/sundarasans38.htm)) |
| A crow keeps pecking at Sita; she tries to stop it with a clod of earth | Sundara 38.15–18 |
| It comes back and tears at her with its claws; she bleeds | Sundara 38.19–23 |
| Ram wakes, sees the blood; the crow is Indra's son | Sundara 38.24–25 |
| Ram takes a blade of kusha grass from the mat and charges it with the Brahma-astra | Sundara 38.26–28 |
| The crow flees through the worlds; his father, the gods and the great sages give him no refuge | Sundara 38.29–32 |
| He returns and falls at Ram's feet | Sundara 38.33 |
| The astra cannot be in vain; the crow gives up his right eye and is spared | Sundara 38.34–37 |
| Sita tells this to Hanuman as a token (abhijnana) only Ram would know | Sundara 38 (and 67, Hanuman retelling it to Ram) |
| "For a crow, a Brahma-astra; why do you bear with the one who took me?" | Sundara 38.39–48 |
| The Part 5 tease: one man against fourteen thousand | Aranya 22–30 (Part 5) |

Not claimed: the name Jayanta (the Ramcharitmanas and later tellings name him; Valmiki calls him the crow, Indra's son);
where exactly she was clawed (the text says her breast; the reel keeps it to "clawed her until she bled" and shows a few
drops on the grass). Performance only: the crown floating over the crow, the glowing grass's comet tail, Indra on
Airavata and a sage on clouds turning him away, the star on "no one", the bubble with the crow and Ravana's head.
