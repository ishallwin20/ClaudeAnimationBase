# Ad 96: "RAM gave everyone JEWELS. HANUMAN asked for this." (Hanuman series, Part 8)

A 92.2 s Painted reel with Bangers captions, no voiceover, retro cartoon SFX and music only as mild beds (warm pads
under the asking, the hug and the blessing, a drone under the parting and the end of Ram's time, a music box under the
ask). Part 8 of the 10-part Hanuman Ji series (`HANUMAN_SERIES.md` on `main`); it stands alone and ends on the no-price
Navadurga card. Part 7's last caption teases it.

```
Logline: Ram is king. The kings' gifts arrive in heaps and he gives them away to Sugriva, Vibhishana and the vanaras.
         When it is time to go home, Hanuman bows and asks for none of it: "let my love for you last, my devotion stay
         steady, my heart go nowhere else. And as long as your story is told on this earth, let me stay alive. Let
         apsaras sing it to me: it will blow away my longing as wind blows away a cloud." Ram gets up from his throne
         and hugs him: "so it will be: you will live as long as my story is told, and it will be told as long as the
         world stands. For each thing you did for me I would give my life." He takes the necklace off his own neck,
         bright as the moon, and ties it on Hanuman. Twist: years later, when Ram's time on earth ends, everyone asks to
         go with him and he says yes; then he turns to Hanuman: "you chose to live. Don't break your promise. As long
         as my story is told, stay, and be happy." And Hanuman was.
Why it should travel: the hook's open question (what do you ask for when everyone is taking jewels?), a wish every
         devotee half-knows, Ram leaving his throne for a hug, and a last beat that turns the boon into a parting.
Hook:    Frame 0: Hanuman kneeling, palms joined, on a plain warm ground; a heap of jewels beside him that he is not
         looking at. Caption up: "RAM GAVE EVERYONE JEWELS." / "HANUMAN" (gold) / "ASKED FOR THIS." 2.6: a "?" bubble.
Arc:     content → humble → longing → held → blessed → adorned → (years) → singled out → glad.
Motif:   Ram's story as a palm-leaf pothi with राम on it, floating over the earth: in his wish, in Ram's "so it will
         be", in Ram's last order, and in Hanuman's lap at the ask.
```

## Facts used
**Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda**, read verse by verse in Sanskrit on 15 Nov 2026 in the edition at
heritage.iitk.ac.in/VRAM/7.<sarga>.<verse> (sarga and verse numbers as there; other editions number these sargas
differently). My own reading of the Sanskrit.
- 39.8–12: the kings, home again, send Ram gifts (horses, elephants, sandalwood, ornaments, gems, pearls, coral);
  Bharata, Lakshmana and Shatrughna bring them to Ayodhya.
- 39.13–15: Ram accepts it all and gives it to Sugriva, to Vibhishana and to the other rakshasas and vanaras with whom
  he won; they wear the jewels he gave on their heads and arms.
- 39.16–19 (not shown): Ram takes Hanuman and Angada on his lap, praises them to Sugriva and ties ornaments from his
  own body on them. So Hanuman too was given ornaments: the reel says only that he did not *ask* for gold.
- 39.26–29: they feast (honey, fruit, roots); over a month passes, and "through devotion to Ram they thought it a
  moment"; a second month passes.
- 40.1–7: Ram tells Sugriva to go to Kishkindha; 40.8: he embraces him again and again; 40.9–11: he tells Vibhishana
  to rule Lanka by dharma.
- 40.14: as they praise Ram, Hanuman bows and speaks (unasked: the text has no offer of a boon).
- 40.15: "let my supreme love stay in you always, O king; let my devotion be steady; let my heart go nowhere else."
- 40.16: "as long as Ram's story goes about on the earth, so long let breath stay in my body."
- 40.17–18: "let apsaras tell me this divine story of yours; hearing the nectar of your deeds I will scatter that
  longing, as the wind a line of cloud."
- 40.19: Ram rises from his throne and embraces him with love.
- 40.20–21: "so it will be, without doubt: as long as my story goes about the world, your fame will last and the
  breath in your body; as long as the worlds stand, my story will stand."
- 40.22: "for each single good deed of yours I would give my life, O monkey." (The edition's next half-line: one
  becomes fit to be repaid only in times of trouble; not used.)
- 40.23–24: he takes from his own neck a necklace bright as the moon, a vaidurya at its centre, and ties it on
  Hanuman, who shines like a golden mountain with the moon on its peak.
- 40.25–29: the vanaras rise, bow and go; Sugriva, Vibhishana, all in tears, leave Ram "as an embodied one leaves the
  body."
- 107.12–16: when Ram resolves to leave the world, the people of Ayodhya ask to follow him wherever he goes; he
  agrees.
- 108.17–22: the vanaras, bears and rakshasas come, led by Sugriva: "we are resolved to follow you"; Ram, smiling,
  agrees. 108.23–26: Sugriva, having crowned Angada, asks the same; Ram: "I would not go without you."
- 108.33–34: to Hanuman: "you set your mind on living: do not make your promise void. As long as my stories go about
  the world, be glad, keeping my word."
- 108.35–36: Hanuman "attained supreme joy" and said: "as long as your purifying story moves in the world, I will
  stay on the earth, keeping your command."

Not claimed: that Ram offered a boon or said "ask for anything" (he does not); that Hanuman refused gifts (39.19: he
was given ornaments); where Hanuman lived between the two scenes; how many years passed ("years later" only); that
*everyone* went with Ram (108.27–32 Vibhishana is told to stay and rule; 108.37 Jambavan, Mainda and Dvivida are told
to live on: the caption names Sugriva, the vanaras and the people of Ayodhya); the gem's English name; "I remain in
your debt" (the popular fuller form of 40.22 is not in the edition read, and a second edition could not be reached).
Corrections to the series plan: the hook ("RAM said: ask for ANYTHING") had no basis in the text and is now "RAM gave
everyone JEWELS. HANUMAN asked for this." (Part 7's teaser was re-rendered to match); the necklace is not "a string of
pearls with an emerald" but one "bright as the moon" with a vaidurya, from Ram's own neck; "and still remain in your
debt" is dropped.
Performance only (no plot claims): the mangoes; the gems on Sugriva's head (39.15 has them worn on heads and arms);
the two crossed-out bubbles; the heart with Ram in it; the pothi over the earth; the grey cloud over Hanuman's head and
the gust; the three bubbles of what he did (the leap, Sita's jewel, the herb mountain: Sundara 1, Sundara 38, Yuddha
74); who walks in the parting shot (Angada and Jambavan are among "the vanaras" and bears); the townspeople's faces;
Hanuman still wearing the necklace years later; the pothi in his lap at the ask.
