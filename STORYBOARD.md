# Ad 55 — "HANUMAN JI has 5 FACES. Only ONE is a monkey." (Panchmukhi Hanuman · Books 1–3)

A ~69 s captioned **Painted** reel, made very cute: no voiceover, Bangers captions, retro cartoon SFX (music added on
Instagram), the Books 1–3 ₹500 card. It tells the Ahiravana legend, how Hanuman got his five faces, then what each face
blesses, and asks the viewer which face they need.

```
Logline: Ravana's brother Ahiravana, the sorcerer-king of Patala, steals the sleeping Ram and Lakshman to sacrifice
         them. Hanuman dives after them, gets past a guard who turns out to be his own son (half vanara, half fish),
         and finds that Ahiravana's life sits in five lamps in five directions, which must all go out at once. One
         mouth, five lamps... so four more faces pop out of his head, and all five blow at once.
Hook:    Frame 0: one subject, Panchmukhi Hanuman (the five faces round his head) on a plain saffron halo;
         "HANUMAN JI HAS / 5 FACES. / Only ONE is a monkey." already up.
Why it should travel: a famous form everyone has seen in temples and on car dashboards, with a story few know ("known
         yet unknown", as with the Kali donkey reel); a cute transformation set piece (four heads popping out one by one);
         a saveable "what each face blesses" slate; an identity-style ask ("Which FACE do YOU need today?"), like ad 28's
         pinned Shani ask.
World:   saffron halo (hook, meaning, ask) → blue night war camp → dawn → down through the earth → teal-violet Patala:
         the gate, then the lamp-lit hall → darkness (the lamps out) → warm light back → saffron again.
Motifs:  THE FIVE FACES (on in the hook, popped away, popped back one by one, the blessing slate). THE LAMPS (five
         flames; one breath). HANUMAN'S TAIL (ties up Makardhwaja).
Ending rhyme: the hook's five-faced Hanuman on the saffron halo comes back for the meaning slate and the ask.
Arcs:    Hanuman: proud smile → shock (empty hut) → determined → baffled ("my SON?") → gentle (ties him up, a pat) →
                  worried (one mouth, five lamps) → eyes closed, glowing → five faces, fierce-cute → happy, carrying
                  them home → winks at us
         Ahiravana: evil grin → smug laugh → jaw drop at the five faces → swirl eyes, gone in a poof
         Makardhwaja: puffed-up guard → proud "son of HANUMAN!" → pouting, tied up → crowned, waving
```

| Makardhwaja is part vanara, part makara | the same |
| They fought; Hanuman beat him and tied him up, then went on | the same |

| Hanuman took five faces, Hanuman, Narasimha, Garuda, Varaha, Hayagriva, and blew out all five at once; Ahiravana died | the same |

| The faces and their directions: Hanuman east, Narasimha south, Garuda west, Varaha north, Hayagriva the sky | Panchamukha Hanumat Kavacham (in the Sudarshana Samhita by tradition; Hindupedia, shlokam.org) |
| What each face grants: Hanuman victory, Narasimha fearlessness, Garuda protection (from poison and evil), Varaha prosperity, Hayagriva knowledge | the same kavacham, as summarised by Hindupedia and shlokam.org |

On screen, the opening is tagged **"From the Ahiravana legend"**. The episode isn't in Valmiki or the Ramcharitmanas; it
comes from the regional Ramayanas (Krittivasi, Ananda) and temple tradition. Where versions differ (brother or son of Ravana,
which goddess, who crowned Makardhwaja), the reel takes Krittivasi's or the widely cited one, or leaves it out: no
goddess is shown, and "became KING of Patala" doesn't say who crowned him. How Ahiravana dies is softened to swirl eyes and a
poof (the tellings have Hanuman behead him). The lamp positions follow the directions on screen: the sky lamp hangs
above, the east lamp stands in front (toward the viewer).
Performance only: the faces' cuteness and expressions, the zzz in the bubble, the fight cloud, the pat on the head, the
counting on fingers, the dropped gada, the crown and the wave.
