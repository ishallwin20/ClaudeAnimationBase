# Reel: "His mom tried to TIE him up. The rope was ALWAYS two fingers short." (Little Kanha, Part 2)

A ~58.4 s captioned Painted reel for followers (ends on a Follow card teasing Part 3). Maa Yashoda is churning curd;
Kanha wants his milk and climbs into her arms, but the milk on the stove boils over and she runs to it. Left alone and
cross, Kanha smashes the curd pot with a stone. Maa comes back to the broken pot and finds him on an upturned mortar,
feeding the butter to the monkeys. She picks up a stick; he runs; she catches him; he cries and rubs his eyes, kajal
everywhere. She throws the stick away and decides to tie him to the mortar instead. The rope is two fingers short. She
ties on another: still two fingers short. Every rope in the house: STILL two fingers. The neighbours laugh; Maa is
tired, sweating, flowers falling from her hair. Seeing her so tired, Kanha lets her tie him: that is why he is
Damodar. Then he drags the mortar between two arjuna trees; they crash down, and two devas step out, freed from Narad
Muni's curse.


## Facts used (Srimad Bhagavatam, Skandha 10)
- Yashoda churned the curd herself (10.9.1–3). Krishna, wanting her milk, caught hold of the churning rod; she took him
  on her lap (10.9.4–5).
- She saw the milk on the stove boiling over, put him down and ran to it (10.9.5).
- Angry, biting his lips, with false tears, he broke the pot of curd with a stone and went off to eat the butter
  (10.9.6). She came back, saw the broken pot (10.9.7) and found him on an upturned mortar, giving butter to the
  monkeys and looking round nervously (10.9.8).
- Seeing her with a stick, he ran; she chased him, the Lord whom even yogis cannot reach by meditation; her hair came
  loose and flowers fell from it (10.9.9–10). Caught, he cried, rubbing his eyes, smearing the kajal (10.9.11).
- She threw the stick away and decided to tie him up instead (10.9.12–14).
- The rope was short by two fingers' width; every rope she joined to it fell short by the same two fingers; the gopis
  smiled in wonder (10.9.15–17).
- She was tired and sweating, the flowers falling from her hair; seeing her effort, Krishna let himself be bound
  (10.9.18); the Lord comes under the control of his devotees (10.9.19).
- The twin arjuna trees were Nalakuvara and Manigriva, Kubera's sons, cursed by Narada for their pride (10.9.22–23).
  Krishna dragged the mortar between them; it caught and the trees fell with a crash; two shining beings came out,
  praised him and left (10.10.23–43).
- The name: Damodara, "the one with a rope (dama) round his belly (udara)", is the name this lila gives him (the
  Damodarashtakam, Padma Purana, is sung to it in the month of Kartik). The meaning of the two fingers (the devotee's
  effort and the Lord's grace) comes from the commentaries, and goes only in the pinned comment, marked as such.
- Performance only, no plot claim: the faces, the sparkles at the gap, the monkeys' chewing, the wink.
