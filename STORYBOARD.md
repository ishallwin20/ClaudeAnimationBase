+ VO (girish_en_devotional, pace 1.3) + Lyria score (take 2 of 3, its first 1.5 s cut, ducked under the voice) + SFX.
77.45 s, 9:16. Built 25 Nov 2026, the day after the Maha Deepam was lit (24 Nov, 6 PM); it burns ~11 days.
Card: the Navadurga Set (no price).

## The idea
Concrete numbers first (the owner: "people love concrete details"), each turned into something you can picture: the
ghee as one home's diya for ~1,000 years, the wick stood up beside the hill, the pot beside a man, the climb. Then the
turn: only fishermen light it, on a mountain 90 km from the sea. Why? The Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam's net-casting episode,
told in moonlit silhouettes: Parvati born a fisherman chief's daughter, the shark, the vow, the net; the fisherman was
Shiva, the shark Nandi. The payoff: the lighters call themselves Parvatha Raja Kulam, so every Karthigai the bride's
family climbs his mountain and lights his fire. Ask: "Who would YOU go see it with? TAG them."

## Facts used
- **Ghee: 3,500 kg.** The Hans India, 25 Nov 2026 (report of the 24 Nov lighting): "a giant cauldron containing 3,500 kg
  of ghee". Consistent with DT Next, 24 Nov 2026 ("about 4,500 liters of ghee", ≈ 4,100 kg) and Free Press Journal,
  23 Nov 2026 ("around 4,500 kg"); Aavin supplied 3.5 t in 2024 (ad 110's notes). (PTI's 2025 report gave 600 kg; not used.)
- **One home's diya for ~1,000 years.** Our arithmetic: 3,500,000 g ÷ 10 g an evening = 350,000 evenings ≈ 958 years. The
  10 g is an assumption for a small ghee diya, shown on screen ("at 10 g of ghee an evening").
- **Wick: 1,500 m of cotton cloth.** DT Next, 24 Nov 2026 ("a 1,500-meter-long cotton cloth wick donated by devotees");
  FPJ, 23 Nov 2026 ("1,500-metre-long cotton wick"); PTI, 3 Dec 2025 ("1,500 metres of cotton cloth that was used as a wick").
- **Nearly twice as tall as the mountain.** The hill is 2,668 ft = 813 m (DT Next, The Hans India); 1,500 ÷ 813 = 1.85.
  The cloth is drawn from sea level, like the 813 m.
- **The pot.** DT Next, 24 Nov 2026: the kopparai "about 5.75 feet in height and weighs 310 kilograms" (vs a ~5'5" man;
  four men of ~70 kg = 280 kg). Painted saffron with "Siva Siva", a linga and Ardhanareeswarar (News Today, 24 Nov 2026;
  the Ardhanareeswarar image is not drawn).
- **Over 40 carry it up.** DT Next, 24 Nov 2026: "Over 40 temple staff and devotees carried this vessel up the 2,668-foot
  hill." The hill has only a footpath ("on foot").
- **The fishermen light it, for generations.** PTI, 3 Dec 2025 ("a clan of fishermen who traditionally light the 'Maha
  Deepam'"); News Today, 24 Nov 2026 ("This honour has traditionally belonged to the Parvatharajakulam community, also known
  as the Sembadavars or Nattars", lit by generations of the community).
- **90 km from the sea.** Tiruvannamalai (79.06 E, 12.24 N) to the nearest coast north of Puducherry ≈ 90 km great-circle.
- **The legend.** Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam (Paranjothi Munivar), Valai Veesiya Padalam (the net-casting chapter, 57th):
  Parvati is born among the fisherfolk as the daughter of their chief; Nandi becomes a shark that troubles the sea; the
  chief promises his daughter to whoever catches it; Shiva comes as a fisherman, casts his net, catches it and marries her
  (Dinamalar temple pages, "Chapter on flinging the net"; Maalaimalar, "Valai veesi arulia thiruvilaiyadal"). Why she was
  born there (a curse) is left out: versions differ.
- **Parvatha Raja Kulam, the family of Parvati's father.** News Today, 24 Nov 2026: Parvati was born as Kayal Kanni,
  daughter of Parvatharajan, a fishing community leader; the community's name Parvatha Rajakulam (Wikipedia, Sembadavar).
  "In their telling" is how the caption frames it ("call themselves").
- **"The bride's family climbs HIS mountain"** is our reading of that claim (if Parvati is their daughter, Shiva is their
  son-in-law), not a quoted tradition.
- **11 days.** DT Next, 24 Nov 2026 ("expected to burn for 11 days").

Kept out: the exact crowd size; "seen from 35 km" (ad 110 used it); the curse's reason; the community's caste politics.
