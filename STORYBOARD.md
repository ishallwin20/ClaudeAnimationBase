# "Why does SHIVA wear such SCARY ornaments?" (ad 78 · 3D)

A 79.3 s explainer in the **3D** style, made from a follower's request (6 Nov 2026): "make a reel on Shiva, guru of all
malas: his hair mala, red thread, wild pig tooth, nar munda mala, rudraksha, karungali, nag mala, tabish…". Researched
first, and the reel teaches only what the texts say: the story behind each of Shiva's ornaments (the snake, the blue
throat, the moon, Varaha's tusk, Gajasura's hide, Ganga in his jata, the skulls of past creators, rudraksha from his own
tears); and crystal, karungali and the red thread, from the follower's list, are what his *devotees* wear (no purana
found that puts them on Shiva). A first cut framed it as "each one was once a THREAT"; the owner dropped that (the tusk
and the skulls were never threats) and asked for the moon. In the shape of the 3D explainers (ads 56, 61, 69, 71): a
dark jewel studio, one ornament at a time, sources on screen, the screenshot board, an identity ask. English VO (Sarvam
`girish_en_devotional`, pace 1.2), karaoke captions, SFX under it (music added on Instagram). Matte: no fx bloom, cine.js
bloom .1. Card: Navadurga Set, no price (as ads 69 and 71).

## Facts used
| claim in the reel | source |
|---|---|
| The king of serpents is his necklace | Shiva Panchakshara Stotram (attr. Adi Shankaracharya), v. 1: *nāgendra-hārāya* "to him whose necklace (hāra) is the king of serpents" ([shlokam.org](https://shlokam.org/shloka/shiva-panchakshara-stotram.htm)); on screen in Devanagari with the gloss |
| When the ocean was churned, halahal poison rose and spread in all directions | Bhagavata Purana 8.7.18 ("it first produced a fiercely dangerous poison called hālahala"), 8.7.19 ("that uncontrollable poison was forcefully spreading up and down in all directions") ([vedabase](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/7/)) |
| Shiva drank it; it turned his throat blue; that blue mark became his ornament | 8.7.42 ("took the whole quantity of poison in his palm and drank it"), 8.7.43 ("marking Lord Śiva's neck with a bluish line. That line, however, is now accepted as an ornament of the Lord") |
| The moon on his head: he wore it for relief from that poison | Skanda Purana, Prabhasa Khanda 1.18 ("The Birth of Moon-God", [wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc626806.html)), Shiva answering Devi (v. 10, "this Candra is on your head"): v. 15 "he was held and kept on the head by me where he still continues to be"; v. 16 "when the poison (Halāhala) was swallowed by me… Candra was formerly made my ornament for the relief (from the effects of poison)"; v. 17 "I came to be known as Śaśibhūṣaṇa (Moon-ornamented)". On screen SHASHIBHUSHANA, "adorned with the moon" |
| This tusk killed the demon Hiranyaksha; it was Varaha's; when it fell, Shiva picked it up and wore it | Linga Purana 1.94 (Shastri, [wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-linga-purana/d/doc1196074.html)): 5–8 "With the tip of his curved fangs he killed Hiraṇyākṣa"; 26b–27 "The curved fangs… pressed down by the weight of the earth fell down there"; 28–32 "Bhava… saw that curved fang and took it up for his own embellishment. Mahādeva kept it on his head as well as on his chest. Hence, Maheśvara wears curved fang (Daṃṣṭrā) as his ornament" |
| Gajasura, the elephant demon, fought Shiva and fell; dying, he asked Shiva to wear his skin; Shiva did; his name: Krittivasa | Shiva Purana, Rudra Samhita, Yuddha Khanda 57 ([wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226198.html)): pierced by the trident (49–50); 58 "wear this hide of mine sanctified by the fire of your trident"; 63 "let your name be Kṛttivāsas (one clad in elephant-hide)"; 64 "Let it be so"; 68 "accepted the hide of Gajāsura and wore it". On screen "Rudra Samhita 5.57" (the Yuddha Khanda is its fifth) |
| The earth could not bear Ganga's fall from heaven; she meant to sweep Shiva away; he held her in his jata | Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 42.24 ("the earth cannot endure the downfall of Ganga… none other than… Shiva"); 43.5–6 ("let me enter netherworld, indeed whisking Shiva with my streams"); 43.6–9 (he pent her up in the tufts of his hair); 43.9–10 she whirled there "for many number of years" ([valmikiramayan.net](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga43/balasans43.htm)). The reel gives no number of years |
| The skulls belong to thousands of past Brahmas and Vishnus | Skanda Purana, Prabhasa Khanda (Prabhasa-kshetra Mahatmya) 9.7, Shiva speaking: "This garland of mine… has been made out of skulls of thousands of Nārāyaṇas and ten thousand Brahmās" ([wisdomlib, The Garland of Skulls and Tattvas](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-skanda-purana/d/doc626796.html)); Vishnu says the same to Bhairava in the Shiva Purana, Shatarudra Samhita 9 ("the garland of the bones of Brahmā shines around your neck", crores of Brahmas in different kalpas, [wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226405.html)). "Vishnus" for Nārāyaṇas |
| Rudraksha grew from Shiva's own tears | Shiva Purana, Vidyeshvara Samhita 25.5–7 ("Drops of tears fell from my beautiful half-closed eyes. From those tear-drops there cropped up the Rudrākṣa plants", [wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc225964.html)); as in ad 56 |
| Crystal, karungali, red thread are worn by his devotees | Crystal (sphatika) is one of the bead materials the Akshamalika Upanishad and the Kurma Purana list for a devotee's japa mala; in the Shiva and Linga Puranas crystal is a *linga* (Linga Purana 1.74, 1.81) and Shiva's crystal-white complexion, not a mala he wears. Karungali is the Tamil name of ebony (Diospyros ebenum), a modern devotional mala. The red thread is the devotee's kalava / raksha sutra. No purana found that puts any of the three on Shiva (checked 6 Nov), so the reel only says devotees wear them |

Checked and not used: the popular story that the skulls are Sati's 108 past births (no scriptural source found; folk); the
popular moon story (Shiva shelters Chandra in his hair after Daksha's curse): Shiva Purana Kotirudra 14 and Skanda
Prabhasa 1.21–22 have Chandra cured at Prabhasa (waning one fortnight, waxing the next) but nothing on the head; only
Prabhasa 1.18 gives a reason, the poison, so the reel uses that; the skulls as Sati's / Parvati's earlier births (107 or
108; blogs and a sectarian site only, no verse); the Darukavana sages' tiger and his tiger skin (no purana passage found);
Vasuki named as his necklace (tradition; the stotra says only "king of serpents"); the follower's hair mala, bell mala
and "tabish" (taweez, an amulet from Islamic tradition): no source puts them on Shiva.

Iconography, no claim: the cobra (a spectacled cobra, hood spread); Shiva's head in the sky camphor-white (*karpūra-gauram*,
the Karpura Gauram stotra's image) so the throat's blue reads; "Neelkantha, the blue-throated one", his epithet; Varaha
"Vishnu as the boar". Performance only: the jewel studio, the poison as a violet stain and smoke, the strands rising to
his mouth, the tusk falling and rising as a crescent, the trishul striking and glowing, the hide falling and spreading
(an elephant's outline from above), the crescent and rudraksha band on the jata, the torrent and the streams winding
round, the skulls hung in a U, the tear falling on a bead and the ring of light.

## Lessons
- A coiled snake's length runs away from you: 2 turns at 40 cm is 5 m of body, so a hood placed by the body's fraction
  spread over a metre. Place the hood (and any detail) by distance from the head along the curve.
- In Chrome a `destination-in` fill clears everything it doesn't cover: to fade a layer's lower part, fill the whole
  canvas with a gradient whose first stop is opaque.
- An elephant's hide doesn't read draped over a rock; spread flat and seen from above, its outline (ears, trunk, legs)
  says "elephant" at once.
- A rudraksha mala seen whole is a dull brown ring; macro on one bead with the ring receding into bokeh is the shot.
