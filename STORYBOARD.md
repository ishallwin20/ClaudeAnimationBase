# Ad 59: "Why did HANUMAN JI bite Maa Sita's PEARLS?" (Painted · Books 1–3)

A ~85 s captioned **Painted** reel, the sequel in spirit to ad 29 (Sindoor), made as adorable as we can: no voiceover,
Bangers captions, cute retro cartoon SFX carrying everything (crunches, plinks, boings, buzzy cartoon voices, gasps).
Music only where it earns it: a soft tanpura and pad under the heart and the hug, nowhere else. Books 1–3 ₹500 card.

## Facts used
**Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda, the coronation sarga** (Critical Edition 6.116.69–73; 6.128 in the Gita Press and
southern editions, 6.131 in some others):
- after Ram's coronation, Sita gives Hanuman spotless divine garments and fine ornaments (69);
- she takes the necklace off her own neck and looks again and again at all the vanaras and at her husband (70);
- Ram, who reads her wish, says: "O lovely one, give the necklace to the one you are pleased with, in whom valour,
  prowess and wisdom always live" (71–72);
- she gives it to the son of Vayu (72); Hanuman shines with that necklace, white as a heap of moonbeams, "like a
  mountain with a white cloud" (73).

**The legend (told as one, like ad 29):** the pearl-biting and the chest-opening are not in Valmiki, the Ramcharitmanas
or a purana. They're the popular devotional story told in homes and temples and retold in devotional and children's
books, and the source of the famous picture of Hanuman with Ram and Sita in his heart. The reel marks the line between
the two: "IT BEGINS IN VALMIKI'S RAMAYANA" for the gift, then "NOW, THE LEGEND EVERY DADI KNOWS" for the rest. The common
telling, beat for beat:
- Hanuman breaks the string and bites the pearls one by one, looking inside each;
- the court is shocked; someone objects that the pearls are priceless (versions differ on who: a courtier, a minister,
  sometimes Vibhishana; the reel keeps it "a courtier", unnamed);
- Hanuman says he's looking for Ram in them, and without Ram they're worthless to him;
- he's asked mockingly whether Ram is inside him, then; he tears open his chest, and Ram and Sita are in his heart;
- Ram embraces him (his chest is whole again).

Performance only (no plot claim): the hook's pearl held up like a telescope, the sniff, the cheeks stuffed like a
squirrel's, the pearl tossed over his shoulder that bonks the courtier's turban, the two vanara friends leaning in
hopefully each time Sita looks their way, Hanuman looking behind himself ("me?"), tiny Ram and Sita waving from inside
his heart, the courtier's turban falling off, and at the end Hanuman peering at Ram through one last pearl.

```
Logline: After Ram's coronation, Maa Sita gave Hanuman her pearl necklace (Valmiki). In the legend every dadi knows,
         he bit the pearls one by one, looking for Ram inside; mocked "is Ram inside YOU, then?", he opened his chest,
         and there they were, Ram and Sita in his heart. That's why Hanuman ji is shown that way.
Hook:    Frame 0: golden Hanuman, close, on a plain peach ground with soft rays, one eye shut, a pearl held up to the
         other like a telescope. Caption up: "WHY DID HANUMAN JI / BITE MAA SITA'S PEARLS?" He sniffs it, pops it in:
         CRUNCH. Chews. Peers into the broken halves: "?". Tosses it over his shoulder: plink, plink, plink.
World:   peach (hook) → Ram's court in morning sandstone (the gift, the biting) → warm gold light out of his heart →
         the court, closer (the hug) → a home mandir at dusk with the famous picture on the wall → peach (the ask) → card.
Motifs:  THE PEARL AT THE EYE (the hook; the bridge into the legend; the last beat, where he sees Ram through it).
         PEARLS as sound: a plink every time one lands, a crunch every bite, a pile that grows.
Rhyme:   opens on Hanuman peering into a pearl and finding nothing; ends on him peering through one and finding Ram.
Arc:     curious → bashful (the gift) → delighted → puzzled → busy (crunch crunch) → stung (the mock) → still → glowing →
         held → cheeky (the wink in the ask).
```
