## Facts used (Brahma Vaivarta Purana, Ganapati Khanda, chs. 11–12; summary: kamakoti.org "Essence of Brahma Vaivarta Purana")
- Parvati's son is born as a beautiful (human-faced) baby; all the gods come to see and bless him.
- Shani comes but keeps his eyes down. Asked why, he explains his wife's curse: he was absorbed in meditation on Krishna
  when his wife (a daughter of the Gandharva Chitraratha) came to him; he never looked at her, and she cursed him that
  whoever he looked at would be destroyed (have his head cut off). Since then he keeps his gaze lowered.
- Parvati, hearing it, insists he look at the child anyway. He glances from the corner of his (left) eye; the baby's
  head is severed and goes to Goloka. Parvati faints.
- Vishnu flies north on Garuda, finds a sleeping elephant (on the Pushpabhadra), brings its head and joins it to the boy.
- Parvati curses Shani to lose his limbs ("angaheen"); the gods plead; she softens it to lameness for ever.
- The better-known version (Shiva beheads the boy guarding Parvati's door) is the Shiva Purana's; the reel names the
  Brahma Vaivarta as its source on screen.
- Iconography (Matsya Purana): Shani's body is dark sapphire blue (indranila); a crow is his mount in popular images.
- Performance only (no plot claim): Shani bonking into a tree with his eyes on his feet, the crow steering him, a flower
  that wilts at his first cursed look (an illustration of the curse), Shiva's relieved shrug at "wasn't it Shiva?".
