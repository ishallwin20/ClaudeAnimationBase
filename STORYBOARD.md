# "SAAT PHERE? The Vedic ritual says THREE rounds. The seven were never rounds. They were STEPS." (ad 102 · 3D)

A 70.8 s explainer in the **3D** style (owner, 18 Nov 2026: "Let's do the SAAT phere one but make sure it's accurate to
whatever source is picked"), for wedding season (Dev Uthani Ekadashi ~20 Nov). It's built in the shape of the top
reels (aarti, Pradakshina P2, Rudrabhishek): something almost every viewer has done or watched, a number, a "did we get
it wrong?" sting and a sourced rule. The ask is a number plus a state call-out ("3, 4 or 7 + your state"). English VO
(Sarvam `girish_en_devotional`, pace 1.2), karaoke captions, SFX under it (music added on Instagram), the Navadurga card
(no price). Matte look, cine.js bloom .1, DOF.

**The source picked: the Paraskara Grihya Sutra** (the Shukla Yajurveda's book of home rites, the wedding in kandikas
1.4–1.8), in Oldenberg's translation (SBE 29, 1886; read on the sacred-texts mirror at neonvagabond.xyz, sbe29160–162).
Every plot beat in the reel is from it. Asvalayana 1.7 and Sankhayana 1.13–14 (same volume) are cited only for the
round count, which they share.

## Facts used
| claim in the reel | source |
|---|---|
| The Vedic rite says three rounds | Paraskara 1.7.3 (he leads her round the fire), 1.7.4 ("twice again, beginning from the fried grain"), 1.7.6 ("after he has led her round the fire three times"); Asvalayana 1.7.6 ("leading her three times round the fire and the water-pot"); Sankhayana 1.13.13, 1.14.2–3 |
| The seven were never rounds; they were steps | Paraskara 1.8.1 (seven steps northward), after the rounds; Asvalayana 1.7.19 and Sankhayana 1.14.5–6 the same (north-east) |
| It's in the Paraskara Grihya Sutra, the wedding rite of the Shukla Yajurveda | Paraskara GS is the Grihya Sutra of the Vajasaneyi (White/Shukla) Yajurveda (Oldenberg's introduction) |
| Two more Grihya Sutras say the same (on screen: THREE ROUNDS, in all three) | Asvalayana 1.7.6; Sankhayana 1.14.2–3. Caveats for the pinned comment: Sankhayana 1.14.4 adds "silently, if they like, a fourth time" (it doesn't say it's a round), Asvalayana 1.7.14 has a fourth grain offering made *without* leading round, and 1.7.15 says some lead her round after each offering |
| Her brother pours puffed rice into her hands; she offers it to the fire | Paraskara 1.6.1 ("The girl's brother pours out of his joined hands into her joined hands fried grain mixed with Sami leaves"), 1.6.2 (she sacrifices it, standing) |
| She steps on a stone: "Be firm like this stone" | Paraskara 1.7.1 (north of the fire, right foot: "Tread on this stone; like a stone be firm") |
| Then they walk round the fire, three times | Paraskara 1.7.3–1.7.6 (the fourth handful of grain, 1.7.5, comes with no round mentioned) |
| Then the seven steps, toward the north; the groom says one word at each | Paraskara 1.8.1 ("He then makes her step forward in a northern direction seven steps (with the words)…") |
| One: food. Two: strength. Three: wealth. Four: joy. Five: cattle. Six: the seasons | Paraskara 1.8.1, Oldenberg: "One for sap, two for juice, three for the prospering of wealth, four for comfort, five for cattle, six for the seasons". *iṣ* (sap/nourishment) is shown as FOOD, *ūrj* (juice/vigour) as STRENGTH, *māyobhava* (comfort/delight) as JOY: the usual English for these words. Asvalayana has "offspring" at five; we follow Paraskara |
| On the seventh he calls her: Friend (on screen: सखे सप्तपदा भव) | Paraskara 1.8.1, "Friend! be with seven steps (united to me)"; Oldenberg's note: Paraskara reads *sakhe saptapadā*, the parallel texts *sakhā saptapadī* |
| Even the Hindu Marriage Act: where there's saptapadi, the marriage is complete at the seventh step | Hindu Marriage Act 1955, s. 7(2): "Where such rites and ceremonies include the Saptapadi (that is, the taking of seven steps by the bridegroom and the bride jointly before the sacred fire), the marriage becomes complete and binding when the seventh step is taken." (quoted on screen) |

Shown but not said: the steps go north from the fire, the bride on the groom's right (no claim), her footprints in
alta, his in turmeric (decoration).

**Left out on purpose:** that seven-round weddings are "wrong" or invalid (the reel never says so; the ask invites
every custom); where the seven-rounds custom came from (no source checked); the popular *saat vachan* (seven promises)
and what each round "means" (not in the sutras; no source checked for their origin); the pole star and Arundhati
(Paraskara 1.8.19, Asvalayana 1.7.22: a good Part 2); the Tamil Nadu amendment (s. 7-A); court cases.

Spoken: "Saat pheray", "Paaraskar", and सप्तपदी written in Devanagari (the owner picked it from four takes: the romanised spellings came out wrong; "rite" was mispronounced, so the hook says "ritual"). Whisper (small) on every take: all right (it hears "Saat pheray" in Hindi mode as
सात फिरे, "Paaraskar" as "Bharaskar", "saptapadi" as "sup the body": Sanskrit words, fine to the ear). The count, the
seven words and FRIEND are measured with silencedetect and patched in `phere_tl.js` (the tool's estimate was up to
0.5 s early), so the steps land on the words.
