# "Why do we MARRY TULSI to a STONE?" (ad 104 · Books 1–3 · Painterly + VO)

A 110.5 s explainer in the **Painterly** style for Tulsi Vivah (Prabodhini Ekadashi Fri 20 Nov 2026, Tulsi Vivah Sat
21 Nov). The shape of the "Why do Hindus…?" explainers: a thing people do at home, the sourced story behind it, a twist,
then the ritual itself and a name call-out. The story follows the **Devi Bhagavatam 9.17–9.25** (the same version as ad
61, Shaligram), where she is Tulasi, Shankhachuda's wife, and her tapas was for Narayana: the curse that makes Vishnu a
stone is also the answer to her prayer ("Her curse… and her prayer… came true, together"). The ask uses the other
telling (Vrinda, wife of Jalandhar: Shiva, Padma and Skanda Puranas): "Which name did YOU grow up with? Comment VRINDA or
TULSI." English VO (Sarvam `girish_en_devotional`, pace 1.25), karaoke captions, SFX and a soft drone under it (music
added on Instagram). Card: the Navadurga card, no price.

## Facts used
| claim in the reel | source |
|---|---|
| Vishnu sleeps four months and wakes on Dev Uthani (Prabodhini) Ekadashi | Chaturmas, Shayani to Prabodhini Ekadashi ([Wikipedia, Prabodhini Ekadashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prabodhini_Ekadashi)) |
| The first wedding of the season is his (Tulsi Vivah opens the wedding season) | "Tulasi Vivaha signifies the end of the monsoon, and the beginning of the wedding season in Hinduism" ([Wikipedia, Tulsi Vivah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsi_Vivah)) |
| Tulasi was a princess, born on Kartik Purnima | DB 9.17 (Vijnanananda tr., [mirror](https://neonvagabond.xyz/mirrors/sacred-texts/hin/db/bk09ch17.htm)): Madhavi, wife of King Dharmadhvaja, "delivered on the full moon night of the month of Kârtik one beautiful daughter"; DB 9.25: her birth ceremony is kept on Kartik Purnima |
| For a lakh divine years she did tapas, wishing for Narayana as her husband | DB 9.17: "There she practised hard Tapasyâ for one lakh divine years. Her main object was to get Nârâyana for her husband" (at Badari) |
| Brahma: first you will marry another; afterwards you will have Narayana | DB 9.17 (Brahma): "You will now be his wife. Afterwards you will get Nârâyana… for your husband" |
| She married Shankhachuda, a danava king; while her vow stayed unbroken death could not touch him | DB 9.22, the voice from the sky: until Vishnu's kavacha is on his neck "and until his wife's chastity be not violated" death cannot touch him (the kavacha, the second condition, is left out, not denied) |
| He took heaven from the gods | DB 9.19: "The Devas, dispossessed of their realms, wandered everywhere like beggars" |
| Shiva went to war against him; Shiva's trishul struck | DB 9.22 (Mahadeva's camp, the battle); DB 9.19: Narayana gives Mahadeva the Śūlāstra, "S'iva will slay the Dânava by this S'ûlâstra" |
| Vishnu went to Tulasi's palace in her husband's form; her vow was broken | DB 9.24: "Bhagavân Hari… took the Kavacha from S'ankhachûda and assuming his form, went to the house of Tulasî" |
| She saw who he really was: "Your heart is like stone… become a stone!" | DB 9.24: she asks "Who are you? O Magician!"; he shows his own form; "Thy heart is like a stone. So let Thee be turned into a stone." |
| Vishnu accepted: you did tapasya for me; now you will have me forever | DB 9.24: "in Vaikuntha you will enjoy me"; "Be like Laksmî"; her tapas for him: Brahmavaivarta, Prakriti Khanda, Vishnu to Tulasi, "For many years you underwent very difficult penances to achieve Me" (as quoted in ad 61's sources) |
| Her body flows as the Gandaki; her hair grows as the tulsi; he lies on her bank as stone, the Shaligram | DB 9.24: "This body of yours will be known by the name of Gandakî… Your hairs will be turned into sacred trees… Tulasî… I will be turned into stone… close to the bank of the river Gandakî" |
| A Shaligram is never worshipped without tulsi | DB 9.24: "He who worships the S'âlagrâma stone without offering Tulasî leaves on it… will have to suffer…" |
| Every Kartik we marry them: a mandap in the courtyard, the Shaligram the groom | Wikipedia, Tulsi Vivah: held between Prabodhini Ekadashi and Kartik Purnima; "A mandapam (wedding booth) is built around the courtyard"; "the groom is… more frequently the shaligrama stone" |
| A red chunari for the bride | the plant dressed as a bride in a red chunari/sari ([hindu-blog](https://www.hindu-blog.com/2008/11/how-to-do-or-perform-tulsi-vivah.html), [smartpuja](https://www.smartpuja.com/blog/tulsi-vivah-2026-date-muhurat-puja-vidhi/)) |
| Families with no daughter give her away as their own kanyadaan | Wikipedia, Tulsi Vivah: "usually borne by a daughter-less couple, who act as the parents of Tulasi"; the kanyadaan "is considered meritorious" |
| Other Puranas call her Vrinda | Vrinda, wife of Jalandhara, in the Shiva, Padma and Skanda Puranas (Wikipedia, Tulsi Vivah); and DB 9.25 names Vrindâ and Vrindâvanî among her eight names |

Checked, not used: the kavacha taken in a brahmin's guise (only a plan in DB 9.19); the number of rounds (it varies by
region: the reel shows the plate going round but the VO names no count); sugarcane (regional; it is drawn as the mandap,
not named).
Performance only (no claim): the vow as a lamp that goes out, the gold shell round Shankhachuda, the silhouettes, Brahma
as a lotus of light, the disguise melting into a figure of blue light that gathers into the stone, Tulasi dissolving into
the river as light, the tulsi leaf landing on the stone.
