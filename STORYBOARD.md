## Facts used
- **The story is a legend, and the reel says so** (the second caption: "THERE'S A LEGEND EVERY DADI KNOWS:"). It is not
  in Valmiki's Ramayana, the Ramcharitmanas or a purana; it is the popular devotional explanation, told in temples and
  homes and retold in devotional books and children's collections. There is no competing scriptural version, so the
  reel tells the common one, beat for beat:
  - after Ram's coronation, in Ayodhya, Hanuman sees Sita putting sindoor in the parting of her hair and asks why;
  - she tells him it is for Shri Ram's long life;
  - Hanuman reasons that if a pinch gives Ram a long life, covering himself in it would do far more, and smears his
    whole body with sindoor;
  - he comes to Ram's court like that, the court laughs, and Ram asks him why;
  - he explains; Ram is moved, embraces him, and blesses him: whoever offers Hanuman sindoor will have his (Ram's)
    blessing. (Versions phrase the blessing differently: wishes fulfilled, troubles removed, dear to Ram; the reel
    uses the plainest: "WILL HAVE MY BLESSING.")
- Practice today: devotees offer Hanuman sindoor mixed with chameli (jasmine) oil, the *chola*, and Tuesdays and
  Saturdays are his days for it. That is why Hanuman murtis are orange.
- Iconography: golden fur (the Hanuman Chalisa's "kanchan baran"), kundals, mukut and gada; Ram blue with the Kodanda;
  Sita in a queen's mukut.
- Performance only (no plot claim): the sneeze of sindoor in the hook, Hanuman tipping matkas of sindoor over his head,
  the tail he forgot and dips last, orange footprints across the court floor, Lakshman laughing hardest, the smudge of
  sindoor left on Ram's cheek after the hug, and the murti's wink at the end.
