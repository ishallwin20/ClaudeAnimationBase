# Ad 94: "RAVANA could lift MOUNTAINS. He couldn't lift LAKSHMAN." (Hanuman series, Part 6)

An 86.7 s Painted reel with Bangers captions, no voiceover, retro cartoon SFX and music only as mild beds (a war drum
under the duel, a low drone under the fall, a gold swell under the twist, a music box under the ask). Part 6 of the
10-part Hanuman Ji series (`HANUMAN_SERIES.md` on `main`); it stands alone and ends on the no-price Navadurga card. Part
5's last caption teased it: "Next: Ravana could lift mountains. He couldn't lift Lakshman."

```
Logline: Ravana comes out to fight himself. Hanuman stops his chariot, right hand raised: "your boon guards you from
         gods and demons, not from monkeys." Palm for palm: Ravana slaps him; he reels, steadies and slaps back, and
         Ravana shakes like a mountain. "Well done, monkey! An enemy worth praising." "Shame on my strength, you're
         still alive." Later Ravana fells Lakshman with Brahma's spear and runs to carry him off: the arms that could
         lift Himalaya, Mandara, Meru and the three worlds cannot lift him. Lakshman remembers he is a part of Vishnu.
         Hanuman's fist lands like a thunderbolt and Ravana drops to his knees. Then Hanuman picks Lakshman up easily
         and takes him to Ram. Twist: too heavy for Ravana, light for Hanuman, and Valmiki gives the reason: friendship
         and devotion. Then he tells Ram: "climb on my back, like Vishnu on Garuda."
Why it should travel: a paradox hook (mountains yes, a man no), a cartoon slap duel with ten heads reacting, Ravana
         praising his enemy (people love it), a cinema-shot punch, and a twist that is a verse ("light because of
         friendship and devotion") anyone can apply to their own people. The ask is a tag.
Hook:    Frame 0: Ravana on a plain dusk field, bent double over Lakshman (lying still), every head straining. Caption
         up: "RAVANA COULD LIFT MOUNTAINS." / "HE COULDN'T LIFT LAKSHMAN." (gold). 2.4: every head pops "!?".
Arc:     proud → sassy → reeling → gleeful → offended → shocked → straining → fierce → tender → proud → loving.
Motif:   weight. Mountains float in Ravana's bubble, Lakshman won't budge, then rises in Hanuman's arms like nothing.
```

## Facts used
**Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda, sarga 59** (verse numbers as at valmikiramayan.net/utf8/yuddha/sarga59, read 14 Nov
2026; translation K. M. K. Murthy):
- 59.5–10: after Prahasta's death Ravana goes to battle himself, in his chariot, and sees the vanara army.
- 59.42–44, 52: he cuts down the vanara leaders with showers of arrows; the vanaras flee.
- 59.53–54: Hanuman rushes at Ravana to stop the rain of arrows, approaches his chariot and raises his right arm.
- 59.55: "Devas, danavas, gandharvas, yakshas and rakshasas cannot slay you (avadhyatvāt); but from monkeys there is
  danger to you."
- 59.56: "This five-branched right hand, raised, will drive out the life long dwelling in your body."
- 59.58: Ravana: "Strike quickly, without fear; win lasting fame; then I will destroy you." (not shown)
- 59.59: "Remember your son Aksha, struck down by me before (prahṛtam… akṣam smara sutam tava)."
- 59.60: Ravana strikes Hanuman on the chest with his palm (talena urasi).
- 59.61: Hanuman reels again and again, steadies in a moment, and strikes Ravana with his palm.
- 59.62: Ravana shakes like a mountain when the earth quakes.
- 59.63: sages, vanaras, siddhas, devas and asuras roar (the cheering vanaras stand for them).
- 59.64: "Sādhu vānara! By your valour you are an enemy worthy of praise."
- 59.65: "Dhig astu mama vīryam… Shame on my strength, that you are still alive, Ravana."
- 59.66–69: "strike me once, my fist will send you to Yama"; Ravana's fist on his chest, Hanuman reels; Ravana turns
  on Nila (left out for time: "later" covers it).
- 59.104, 107–109: Lakshman, struck on the forehead by Brahma's arrow, breaks Ravana's bow; Ravana takes up a spear
  given by Brahma (svayambhu-dattā), blazing, smoky, like fire, and hurls it; Lakshman strikes at it with arrows, but
  it enters his chest.
- 59.110: Lakshman lies on the ground blazing (jajvāla); Ravana rushes up and seizes him with both arms.
- 59.111: "Able to lift Himavan, Mandara, Meru, or the three worlds with the gods, he could not lift Bharata's younger
  brother."
- 59.112: struck by Brahma's spear, Lakshman remembered himself as an inconceivable part of Vishnu.
- 59.113: pressing him with his arms, Ravana could not move him.
- 59.114–115: Hanuman, enraged, rushes at Ravana and strikes his chest with a fist like a vajra; Ravana reels and
  falls to the ground on his knees.
- 59.116: he sits motionless in his chariot (the blood from his face, eyes and ears is not shown: dazed stars instead).
- 59.118: Hanuman takes Lakshman in his arms and brings him to Ram.
- 59.119: Lakshman, whom his foes could not move, became light for Hanuman through his friendship (suhṛttvena) and
  supreme devotion (bhaktyā paramayā).
- 59.120, 122: the spear leaves Lakshman and returns to Ravana's chariot; Lakshman is healed (his eyes open in the
  twist).
- 59.124–126: "Climb on my back and punish the demon, as Vishnu rides Garuda"; Ram climbs on Hanuman.

Not claimed: a reason for Ravana's failure beyond the text's own next verse (112 is shown as a separate beat, in the
text's words, with no "because"); what happens after (Ravana's arrows on Hanuman, 134–135; Ram breaks his chariot,
cuts his crown and sends him home to rest, 137–144: left out for time); whether the duel was fought from the chariot
(the reel stages it on the ground).
Performance only (no plot claims): the ten heads' faces, the gulps, steam and red faces, the swirl eyes and stars,
the twenty arms clapping (Ravana's praise is in the text, the clapping is not), the thumb, the pout at his own palm,
the slipping feet, the "!?" emotes, the hearts, the bubbles, the arrow bouncing off the spear (109 has him strike at
it), Ram's bow drawn as he rides.
