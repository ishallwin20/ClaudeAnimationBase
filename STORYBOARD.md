# Ganga fell from heaven hard enough to break the earth. Shiva's answer? His HAIR. (reel/ganga-jata)

A Cinematic reel (see CINEMATIC_GUIDE.md), 81.3 s, 9:16, with a voiceover: the English narrator from the Bhramari reel
(Sarvam `girish_en_devotional`, pace 1.2), Ganga's one line in `aditi_en_stories`, a synthesized SFX track and a Lyria
score ducked under the voice. It ends on a follow card with no offer. It's a "known yet unknown" explainer: everyone
has seen Ganga in Shiva's hair; few know the 60,000 princes, the sage who burnt them, the king who stood on one toe,
or the sage who drank the river.

```
Logline: King Sagara's 60,000 sons hunt their father's stolen horse, dig down to the underworld and find it beside
         the sage Kapila. They call him a thief; with one sound he burns them to ash. Only Ganga, the river of heaven,
         can free their souls. Kings fail for generations, until Bhagiratha prays for thousands of years and Brahma
         agrees, but the earth can't bear her fall. Only Shiva can. Bhagiratha stands on one toe for a year. Ganga,
         proud, means to sweep Shiva into the underworld; he catches her in his hair and she's lost in it for years.
         He lets out seven streams; one follows Bhagiratha's chariot. The sage Jahnu drinks her whole for flooding his
         yagna, then lets her out through his ear. She reaches the ashes and 60,000 souls rise to heaven. That's why
         Shiva is Gangadhara, and Ganga is Bhagirathi and Jahnavi.
Why it should travel: the winning shape of ad 15 and Bhramari (an impossible force, then the god's teased "answer":
         ONE TOE, SIX LEGS, HIS HAIR); a famous image explained; three spectacles nobody has seen animated (a river
         vanishing into hair, a king on one toe, a sage drinking a river); a NAME call-out with names many viewers have.
Comment: "Is your name GANGA or JAHNAVI? Comment it!"
Pinned:  "Where did YOU take your first dip in Ganga? Haridwar, Rishikesh, Kashi, Prayagraj? Comment your ghat 👇"
```

## Facts used (every plot beat, with its source)
- Sagara, king of Ayodhya, had 60,000 sons by Sumati: Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 38.
- His Ashvamedha horse was stolen (by Indra, in disguise); the sons dug through the earth to find it: Bala Kanda 39.
- They found it grazing near the sage Kapila (Vasudeva in the form of Kapila), rushed at him calling him a thief, and he
  burnt them to ash with one sound ("hum"): Bala Kanda 40. (Bhagavata Purana 9.8 tells it the same way.)
- Garuda told Anshuman that only the waters of Ganga, from heaven, could take them to heaven: Bala Kanda 41.
- Anshuman, then Dilipa, did tapas and failed: Bala Kanda 42.
- Bhagiratha did tapas at Gokarna for thousands of years (arms raised, five fires, one meal a month); Brahma granted
  Ganga, but warned that the earth could not bear her fall and only Shiva could hold her: Bala Kanda 42. Bhagavata
  Purana 9.9.4–6 has Ganga ask who will hold her force, or she'll break through the earth into Rasatala (the hook's
  "hard enough to break the earth").
- Bhagiratha worshipped Shiva for a year, standing on the tip of one toe: Bala Kanda 43.1–2.
- Ganga, proud, thought she would sweep Shankara down into Patala with her current; Shiva caught her in his matted hair
  and she wandered lost in it for years, unable to find a way out: Bala Kanda 43.
- Shiva released her (into Bindu lake) in seven streams; three went east, three west, and the seventh followed
  Bhagiratha's chariot: Bala Kanda 43.
- She flooded the sage Jahnu's sacrificial ground; he drank all her waters, and at the gods' prayer released her
  through his ears, so she became his daughter, Jahnavi: Bala Kanda 43.
- She reached the ocean and Rasatala, washed over the ashes, and the 60,000 went to heaven; Brahma named her Bhagirathi:
  Bala Kanda 44.
- Gangadhara ("who holds Ganga") is one of Shiva's names and the classic form of him with Ganga in his jata
  (Shiva Sahasranama; the Gangadhara murtis at Ellora and Elephanta).

Not claimed: Ganga's makara and her look (iconography only), and the score's "Har Har Gange" (a chant, not a story beat).

## Sound
- SFX: wind beds; a rising torrent roar for each fall (new `torrent()` in `tools/sfx.mjs`); a drum boom and sub drop on
  "His HAIR" and "ASH"; `om` drone under the tapas; `fire` under the five fires and the yagna; a gulp (`gurgle`) for
  Jahnu; chimes as the souls rise; a soft conch on GANGADHARA.
- Score: Lyria, pinned form (tempo, bars, length at the top and the end); a hush at the catch, made in the mix if Lyria
  plays through it; "Har Har Gange" sung once at the souls rising.
