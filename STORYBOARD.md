# Ad 93: "HANUMAN offered to carry SITA home. She said NO." (Hanuman series, Part 5)

An 87.6 s Painted reel with Bangers captions, no voiceover, retro cartoon SFX and music only as mild beds (a pad and
drone in the grove, a drone and harp under her two hard reasons, a sunrise swell for Ram, a music box under the ask).
Part 5 of the 10-part Hanuman Ji series (`HANUMAN_SERIES.md` on `main`); it stands alone, and ends on the no-price
Navadurga card. Part 4's last caption teased it: "Next: Hanuman offered to carry Sita home. She said no."

```
Logline: In the Ashoka grove Hanuman gives Sita Ram's ring, then offers to carry her home on his back ("I could carry
         all of Lanka, with Ravana in it!"). She laughs at his small body; he grows like Mount Meru, blazing like
         fire. She knows his strength, and still says no, for four reasons: she could faint and fall into the sea;
         demons would chase them, and he can't fight and guard her at once; she will not by choice touch any man but
         Ram (Ravana seized her by force); and Ram must come himself, defeat Ravana and take her home: that is worthy
         of him. Hanuman is delighted: every word is right. "Tell Ram I can hold on one more month." She gives him
         her hair jewel; it's too small for his arm, so he wears it on his finger.
Why it should travel: a "wait, WHAT?" hook (she said no to being rescued), a cute power-up (the small monkey who
         becomes a mountain), four numbered reasons people can argue over, a twist that turns the "no" into faith in
         Ram, an emotional line ("one more month"), and a cute last gag (the jewel on his finger). The ask is a
         yes/no anyone can answer.
Hook:    Frame 0: Hanuman alone in the night grove, grinning, his hand out at his shoulder: hop on. Caption up:
         "HANUMAN OFFERED TO CARRY / SITA HOME." / "SHE SAID NO." (gold). 2.5 "no, no" off screen: his face falls, "?".
Arc:     eager → proud → hurt → mighty → puzzled → listening (fear, danger, dignity, grief, hope) → delighted →
         sad → treasured → silly-happy.
Motif:   the numbered badges 1–4 on her reasons; the bubble from her head holds each one.
```

- Next-part tease: Ravana straining over Lakshman, who lies on the battlefield.

## Facts used
**Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda** (verse numbers as at valmikiramayan.net/utf8/sundara/sarga36–38, read 13 Nov 2026;
translation K. M. K. Murthy):
- 36.2–3: "Look at this ring, marked with Rama's name… given by Rama, to gain your confidence."
- 36.4 (gloss): she takes it, looks at it and is glad, as if she had reached her husband (bhartāram iva samprāptā).
- 37.19, 37.24: "Ascend my back. I shall take you to Rama." "Mount on my back. Do not hesitate."
- 37.20 (and 37.37): "I have the capacity to carry even Lanka, together with Ravana."
- 37.29–30: "How do you wish to carry me so far? I consider this your apishness (kapitvam)." "With such a small body,
  how do you wish to take me from here?"
- 37.31: he thought it the first insult done to him.
- 37.32–36: he grows to give her confidence, shining like Meru and Mandara, blazing like fire, red-faced, with teeth
  and nails hard as vajra, terrible to see, like a mountain.
- 37.40: "I know fully well your courage and strength."
- 37.43–45: "Your speed, like the wind's, may bewilder my mind… I will fall from your back out of fear… fallen
  senseless into the sea, I may become food for its creatures."
- 37.46–50: "a risk for you, with a woman to protect as well"; demons with darts and clubs will surround you; "how can
  you go on while protecting me?"; "stricken with fear, I may fall from your back."
- 37.51–56: they may defeat you, take me back to Lanka or kill me; your effort would be in vain (left out of the reel
  for time; reason 2 carries the danger).
- 37.60: "I cannot touch the body of any man other than Rama."
- 37.61: Ravana's touch was by force: "helpless, out of my own control, with no protector, I could do nothing."
- 37.55, 57, 62, 66: if you alone kill the demons, Rama's fame would be lessened; there is great merit in Rama coming
  here with you; if Rama kills Ravana and takes me back, that would be proper for him; bring my husband here soon.
- 38.1–3: Hanuman was very pleased: "What you spoke is befitting, devi… it is not fit for a woman to cross the ocean on
  my back."
- 38.67: "I shall hold on to my life for a month. After a month I cannot survive; I tell you the truth."
- 38.69: she untied the bright jewel for her head, tied in her garment, and gave it to Hanuman.
- 38.70: he fitted it on his finger, since his arm would not go into it (even in his small form).

Not claimed: that the jewel fit "only" his finger or his little finger (the text says his arm would not go through
it); a reason for the month; the crow story she tells as a token (38.12–38, left out for time); Sita's exact posture
(she sits on the bare ground, 15.30: the rise hides her lower half).
Performance only (no plot claims): the "hop on" gesture and back pats, Sita measuring "this small" with her fingers
and laughing (the text gives her words, not her manner), the anger mark, the sparkles as he grows, the clouds round his
knees (a picture of "like a mountain"), the shrink back with a poof (the text has him take his small form again before
taking the jewel, 38.70's note), the fish, the arrows, the bubbles and badges, the sunrise, the crescent moon, the
squeeze and the "ta-da", the YES / NO pills.
