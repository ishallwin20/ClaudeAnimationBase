# "There are SEVEN worlds below your feet. And not one of them is HELL." (ad 79 · 3D)

An 84.7 s explainer in the **3D** style (owner, 6 Nov 2026: "I learnt that there are 7 layers to nether world… 3D
explainer style… viral worthy, a strong hook"). The hook turns a belief round: people hear *Patal* and think hell; the
Puranas call the seven lower worlds *bila-svarga*, heavens in the earth, richer than Indra's. Then a dive down a cut-
away column of the earth, one cavern per world, each with the one fact the Bhagavata gives about it, and below them all
Ananta Shesha with the universe on one hood like a mustard seed. The screenshot board (the seven with who lives there),
a pick-one ask ("Which world would YOU visit?"), the Navadurga card (no price, as ads 69, 71, 78).

English VO (Sarvam `girish_en_devotional`, pace 1.2), karaoke captions, SFX under it (music added on Instagram). Matte:
no fx bloom, cine.js bloom .12, DOF and a gentle grade (owner, 30 Oct).

Names are spelled and spoken the way Hindi speakers say them (Atal, Vital, Sutal, Talatal, Mahatal, Rasatal, Patal; owner
on HALAHAL, 6 Nov), with the Devanagari under each title.

## Facts used
| claim in the reel | source |
|---|---|
| Seven worlds below the earth: Atala, Vitala, Sutala, Talatala, Mahatala, Rasatala, Patala (in that order) | Bhagavata Purana 5.24.7 ("beneath this earth are seven other planets, known as Atala, Vitala, Sutala, Talātala, Mahātala, Rasātala and Pātāla", [vedabase](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/24/)) |
| Not one of them is hell | 5.24.8 calls them *bila-svarga*, "subterranean heavens… even more opulent than those in the higher planets"; the hells (naraka) are a separate set of places, 5.26 (and Vishnu Purana 2.6) |
| Narada went down there and said they were more beautiful than Indra's heaven | Vishnu Purana 2.5 (Wilson, [wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/vishnu-purana-wilson/d/doc115965.html)): "The Muni Nārada, after his return from those regions to the skies, declared amongst the celestials that Pātāla was much more delightful than Indra's heaven" |
| Each 10,000 yojanas deep (the depth labels 10,000 … 70,000) | Vishnu Purana 2.5: "Its depth below the surface is said to be seventy thousand Yojanas, each of the seven regions of Pātāla extending downwards ten thousand" (also the summary to Bhagavata 5.24) |
| Atal: Bala, the son of Maya, made 96 kinds of magic | Bhagavata 5.24.16: "In Atala there is a demon, the son of Maya Dānava named Bala, who created ninety-six kinds of mystic power" (the rest of the verse, the women and the intoxicant, left out) |
| Vital: Shiva lives here as the lord of gold (Hatakeshvara) | 5.24.17: "Vitala, wherein Lord Śiva, who is known as the master of gold mines (*hāṭakeśvara*), lives with his personal associates"; the river Hāṭakī and the gold called Hāṭaka (shown as the river of gold; the verse's account of how the gold forms left out) |
| Sutal, the world of King Bali; Vishnu himself stands guard at his door, mace in hand | 5.24.18 (Vamana took the three worlds and gave Bali Sutala); 5.24.27: "The Supreme Personality of Godhead… stands with club in hand at Bali Mahārāja's door" |
| Talatal: Maya, the demon architect; Shiva once burned his three cities, then, pleased with him, gave his kingdom back | 5.24.28: "Talātala… ruled by the Dānava demon named Maya"; "Lord Śiva, who is known as Tripurāri, once set fire to the three kingdoms of Maya, but later, being pleased with him, he returned his kingdom"; 5.24.9 Maya Danava "an expert artist and architect" who built their cities |
| Mahatal: serpents with many hoods, always afraid of Garuda | 5.24.29: "the abode of many-hooded snakes, descendants of Kadrū… always disturbed by fear of Garuḍa" |
| Rasatal: demons, enemies of the gods, hiding in holes, in fear of Indra | 5.24.30: "Paṇis, Nivāta-kavacas, Kāleyas and Hiraṇya-puravāsīs… all enemies of the demigods, and they reside in holes like snakes"; they fear Indra when his messenger Saramā chants a curse (shown as a flash from above) |
| Patal: Vasuki rules the Nagas; no sun shines here; the jewels on their hoods light everything | 5.24.31: Nagaloka, "the chief among them is Vāsuki"; "the light emanating from the gems illuminates the entire planetary system of bila-svarga"; 5.24.11 "there is no sunshine in those subterranean planets"; 5.24.12 the gems on the serpents' hoods dissipate the darkness |
| Below all seven, Ananta Shesha holds the whole universe on one hood, like a mustard seed | 5.25.1 (Ananta lives below Pātāla); 5.25.2: "This great universe, situated on one of Lord Anantadeva's thousands of hoods, appears just like a white mustard seed" |

Where the texts differ: the Vishnu Purana names the seven Atala, Vitala, Nitala, Gabhastimat, Mahatala, Sutala, Patala
(Wilson notes the Bhagavata and Padma differ); the reel follows the Bhagavata's names and order and says so on the board.
The Vishnu Purana's Narada also says the sun there gives light without heat, where the Bhagavata says there is no sun;
the "no sun" line is the Bhagavata's (5.24.11) and its tag is on screen. Both texts agree on the jewels' light.

Iconography, no claim: the earth as a column with a quarter cut away; the palaces in every world (Maya built their
cities, 5.24.9); Vishnu as a four-armed figure in dark blue with crown, chakra, conch and mace, in front of the lit door;
the three floating cities and Shiva's fire arrow (Tripura's burning, told as an arrow in the Shiva Purana; the reel only
says "burned"); Garuda as a passing shadow; the demons as red eyes and spear tips in the pits; Indra as a flash; the
universe as a golden egg (Brahmanda) with the seven worlds inside, on Shesha's hood.

Speech-to-text (Sarvam saarika:v2.5) on every take: all lines come back right ("Sutal" hears as "Sudal", the dental t). "Vital" was read as the English word (STT "वाइटल"); the voice now reads "Vithal" (a take STT hears as "विटल"; takes vary, so the take was picked);
"who thinks Paataal" ran together as "Spartyle" until a comma went after "thinks".
