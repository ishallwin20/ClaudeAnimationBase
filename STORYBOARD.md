# Ad 95: "BHARAT shot HANUMAN out of the sky" (Hanuman series, Part 7)

A 92.8 s Painted reel with Bangers captions, no voiceover, retro cartoon SFX and music only as mild beds (a drone under
the grief, a warm pad under the prayer and the twist, a music box under the ask). Part 7 of the 10-part Hanuman Ji
series (`HANUMAN_SERIES.md` on `main`); it stands alone and ends on the no-price Navadurga card. Part 6's last caption
teased it. This is the series' one Ramcharitmanas episode: the text is named in the opening's source line, in the
twist's caption ("Tulsidas writes") and in the Instagram caption (Valmiki does not have the episode).

```
Logline: Lakshman lies struck by Meghnad's spear; the healer names a herb on a far mountain. Ravana sends Kalanemi,
         who sits on the road as a sage beside a lake he has conjured: "bathe, then I'll teach you." In the lake a
         crocodile seizes Hanuman's foot; he strikes it and it rises as an apsara freed from a curse: "that is no sage,
         he's a demon." "Guru ji, your fee first": one swing of his tail. He cannot tell the herb, so he takes the
         mountain, and flies over Ayodhya at night. Bharat sees a giant shape, takes it for a demon and shoots an arrow
         with no tip. Hanuman falls saying "Ram, Ram". Bharat hears the name, runs, holds him; he will not wake. "If my
         love for Ram is true, let him wake, free of pain": he sits up. Bharat hears the news and weeps: "I could not
         serve Ram even once"; then offers to shoot him to Ram on his own arrow, mountain and all. For a moment
         Hanuman is proud ("can an arrow carry my weight?"), then he thinks of Ram and bows. Twist: all the way back he
         keeps praising Bharat's love for Ram.
Why it should travel: a hook nobody expects (Ram's own brother shoots Hanuman), a comic first half (fake sage with a
         halo and horns, a crocodile, a tail wham), a silent cinema beat (the draw, the twang, the fall with "Ram…
         Ram…"), a prayer that works, Hanuman being humbled, and an identity ask every family can answer.
Hook:    Frame 0: night over Ayodhya's roofs, Hanuman flying with the mountain, the tipless arrow already rising at
         him. Caption up: "BHARAT SHOT" / "HANUMAN" (gold) / "OUT OF THE SKY." 2.6: it strikes, "!?".
Arc:     eager → fooled → startled → cross → smug → struck → still → woken → moved → proud → humbled → loving.
Motif:   the name. "Ram… Ram…" rises from him as he falls; it is what makes Bharat run, and what bows Hanuman's head.
```

## Facts used
**Tulsidas, Ramcharitmanas, Lanka Kand** (Awadhi text read 15 Nov 2026 in the swargarohan.org edition, dohas numbered
54–61 there; my own reading of the Awadhi, chaupai numbers as "doha.chaupai" for the lines that follow that doha's
predecessor, as the edition prints them):
- 54 (chaupai before the doha): Meghnad lets fly the shakti; it strikes Lakshman's chest; he faints.
- 55.3–4: Hanuman brings him to Ram, who grieves; Jambavan names the physician Sushena, who lives in Lanka; Hanuman
  fetches him, house and all (not shown). Doha 55: Sushena names the mountain and the herb; "go, son of the wind".
- 56.1–2: a spy tells Ravana, who goes to Kalanemi's house; Kalanemi: "who can stop the one who burnt your city before
  your eyes?" (his bubble). Doha 56: Ravana is furious; Kalanemi decides to go (his advice to worship Ram, 56.3–4, and
  his thought that dying at Ram's messenger's hand is better, are left out for time).
- 57.1–2: he makes maya on the way: a lake, a temple, a fine garden; Hanuman sees a good ashram and thinks to ask the
  muni for water; the demon sits there in a false guise; Hanuman bows.
- 57.4: Hanuman asks for water, is given the kamandalu, says a little water won't do; "bathe in the lake and come
  back quickly; I will give you diksha, so that you gain knowledge" (the reel: "then I'll teach you").
- Doha 57: as he enters the lake a she-crocodile (makari) seizes his foot; he kills her; she takes a divine body and
  goes up to the sky (the reel shows one blow and no death: "one blow… and she rose as an apsara").
- 58.1–2: "seeing you I am free of sin; the great sage's curse is gone. This is no sage: a dreadful demon." So saying
  the apsara leaves.
- 58.2–3: Hanuman: "Muni, take your guru-dakshina first; give me the mantra afterwards." He winds his tail round the
  demon's head and dashes him down; dying, he shows his own form (he dies saying "Ram, Ram": not shown; the reel shows
  him flat, seeing stars).
- 58.4: he sees the mountain, cannot recognise the herb, uproots the mountain at once; holding it he runs through the
  night sky and passes over Avadhpur.
- Doha 58: Bharat sees something very huge, guesses a demon, draws the bow to his ear and shoots an arrow without a
  head (binu phar sayak).
- 59.1: struck, he falls to the ground in a faint, remembering "Ram, Ram, Raghunayak"; hearing the dear words Bharat
  runs to him.
- 59.2: seeing him in distress he presses him to his heart; he does not wake, however he tries; sad-faced, eyes full
  of tears, he speaks.
- 59.3–4: "…if in thought, word and body my love for Ram's lotus feet is without guile, and if Raghupati is kind to
  me, let the monkey be free of weariness and pain." Hearing it, Hanuman sits up, saying "victory, victory to the lord
  of Kosala". Soratha 59: Bharat holds him to his heart, thrilled, eyes wet.
- 60.1–2: Bharat asks after Ram, Lakshman and Sita; Hanuman tells it all in brief; Bharat grieves: "alas, why was I
  born into the world? I was of no use to the Lord in even one task."
- 60.3: "You will be late going, and at dawn the task is lost. Climb on my arrow with the mountain; I will send you
  where the abode of mercy is."
- 60.4: hearing it, pride rises in Hanuman's mind: "how will the arrow go with my weight?"; then, thinking of Ram's
  power, he bows at Bharat's feet with joined palms.
- Doha 60: "keeping your might in my heart, lord, I will go at once"; he takes leave and goes, praising again and
  again in his mind Bharat's strength of arm, goodness, virtues and boundless love for the Lord's feet.
- Soratha 61, 62.1: Hanuman arrives; Ram embraces him; the physician gives the remedy; Lakshman sits up, glad.

Not claimed: why the arrow had no head (the text does not say); whether Hanuman rode the arrow (the text has him
take leave and go: the reel says "he flew on"); that Valmiki tells this (he does not: the Instagram caption says so);
which sage cursed the apsara, or her name; where in Ayodhya Bharat was.
Corrections to the series plan: the dohas are 54–61 in the edition read (the plan said 52–60); it is the freed apsara,
not the crocodile, who warns him; the plan's ask ("Valmiki or Tulsidas: which Ramayan did you grow up with?") was used
by ad 67, so the ask is now "the Lakshman of your family, or the Bharat?".
Performance only (no plot claims): the halo, the little horns and the fang on the disguise; Kalanemi's sweat; the
"BONK", "WHAM", "TWANG" and "THUNK"; the halo rolling off; Hanuman dusting his hands; Bharat's thought bubble of a
demon; the shadow over the garden; the gold light over Hanuman during the prayer; the bubbles of the arrow ride and
the arrow snapping; Ram's image when Hanuman thinks of him; Ram in tears beside Lakshman in the opening (his lament
is 61.1–8); the ask's two pictures (Lakshman going with Ram; Bharat before Ram's sandals: Ayodhya Kand).
