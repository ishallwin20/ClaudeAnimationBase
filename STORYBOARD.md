# Maa Tara and Shiva (Painterly, collab)

A collab reel with @shagunshrotriyaparashar_: her Hindi voiceover (1:48, `assets/vo/tara_vo.mp3`, kept out of git),
our picture. Painterly style, night sets only (dark, not a dreamy painted sky: the AI-label warning in the guide).
Subtitles are the VO transliterated into Roman Hindi (not translated), karaoke chunks, names in gold.
Ends on a "follow both" card. ~112 s.

## Facts used

- Shiva holds the halahala from the churning of the ocean for the good of all, and his throat turns blue: Bhagavata
  Purana 8.7 (the churning; Shiva drinks the poison, it stays in his throat, Nilakantha); Shiva Purana.
- Tara (a form of the Devi: Durga / Sati / Parvati in the tellings) takes the poisoned Shiva on her lap as her child and
  nurses him, and her milk cools the poison: the legend of Tarapith (Birbhum, West Bengal), the temple's own tradition.
  The original stone image in the sanctum shows Tara as a mother suckling Shiva (it is covered by the metal image
  devotees usually see). The temple's other legend (Vasishtha's tapas, Tara appearing to him in that same form) agrees.
  This is a sthala purana / Shakta tradition, NOT the Bhagavata's account; the reel says so on screen ("As worshipped
  at Tarapith, Bengal"), the caption says so, and the pinned comment explains it.
- Tara's look: blue-black skin, the third eye, loose hair, a crown, the khadga and the blue lotus (utpala), a tiger skin
  (Mahavidya iconography). Kept gentle for a karuna story: no tongue out, a garland of red jaba (hibiscus, the flower
  offered at Tarapith) instead of the garland of heads.

## How the nursing is shown (owner: "baby Shiva is fine, don't show much")

Her lap takes a form of light; at "ek shishu maana" the light shrinks into a baby Shiva wrapped in her pallu, only his
face showing at her chest. The milk is a stream of white light into his blue throat; the blue fades, he smiles and sleeps.
No anatomy anywhere.

## Sound

The VO on top. A mild Lyria bed (tanpura, bansuri, soft strings, a lullaby feel), side-chain ducked under the voice,
never loud. Quiet SFX: the sea churning, the poison's hiss, a heartbeat slowing, the change (a shimmer and a bell),
the river and crickets, temple bells, a chime as the poison clears.
