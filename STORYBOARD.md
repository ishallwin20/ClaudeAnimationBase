## Facts used
- The sage Mrikandu (Mrikanda) and his wife did tapas to Shiva for a son. Shiva offered a choice: a son of low intellect
  with a long life, or a virtuous, wise son who would live only sixteen years. They chose the wise son, Markandeya
  (the Puranic retelling, as told at Thirukkadaiyur; see Markandeya in Vyasa Online and Wikipedia).
- On the day he was to die, Markandeya was worshipping Shiva in the linga. Yama's messengers could not take him.
- Yama came himself and threw his noose; it fell round Markandeya and the linga he embraced.
- Shiva emerged from the linga in fury and struck Yama (with his foot, the Kalasamhara / Kalantaka form; Tevaram's
  Atta-virattam, the eight sites of Shiva's heroic acts, Thirukkadavur being the one where he kicked Kala), killing him.
- With Yama dead, nobody died; Bhudevi could not bear the burden; the devas, led by Vishnu, begged Shiva to revive Yama.
  Shiva revived him, on the condition that Markandeya would live forever (sixteen forever, a chiranjivi)
  (Amritaghateswarar-Abirami temple, Thirukkadaiyur, sthala purana).
- Iconography, no plot claim: Yama's buffalo, crown, red eyes, noose and mace; Shiva's third eye, trishul, jata.
- Performance and image only, no plot claim: the two lamps (a picture of the choice), the lamps the boy prays by, the
  Yamadutas' shapes, Yama's shadow on the wall, the cracking stone, the crown and mace flying, the crowd to the horizon,
  Yama waking at dawn; "Om Namah Shivaya" as the boy's prayer (the Shaiva panchakshara, not a claim about his words).
