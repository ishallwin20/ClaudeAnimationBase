# Ad 24: "She OUTSMARTED YAMRAJ with ONE wish." (Savitri and Satyavan)

A ~60 s captioned Painted reel (Bangers captions, SFX only) ending on the 15K Family Deal card (Books 1–3, ₹350, till
25 Oct). The Mahabharata's telling (Vana Parva, the Pativrata-mahatmya, 277–283): Princess Savitri chooses Satyavan, a
prince living in the forest. Narad Muni warns that he will die in one year; she won't choose again. On the last day
Satyavan's head aches, he lies in her lap, and Yama comes, draws out his thumb-sized soul with his noose and walks south.
Savitri follows. He tells her to go back; she keeps walking and speaks of dharma; he grants boons, anything but his
life: her blind father-in-law's sight, his lost kingdom, a hundred sons for her father. Then she asks for a hundred sons
of her own, by Satyavan. "So be it!" "You gave me a hundred sons… and you're taking my husband?" Yama, pleased, sets
him free, with four hundred more years. That's why wives keep Vat Savitri.

## Facts used (Mahabharata, Vana Parva 277–283, the Savitri-upakhyana; critical edition numbering)
- Savitri, daughter of King Ashvapati of Madra, was sent to find a husband herself, and chose Satyavan, son of the blind
  King Dyumatsena, who had lost his kingdom and lived in a forest hermitage (277–278).
- Narada told Ashvapati that Satyavan had every virtue but one flaw: he would die one year from that day. Asked to choose
  again, Savitri said she had chosen once and would not choose a second time (278).
- Married, she lived in the hermitage and counted the days; she kept a three-night fast before the day (279).
- On the day she went with him into the forest; splitting wood, he felt a pain in his head and lay down with his head in
  her lap (280).
- Yama came: dark yet shining, red-eyed, in red garments, crowned, a noose in his hand. He drew out of Satyavan's body a
  person the size of a thumb, bound with his noose, and went south (281).
- Savitri followed. Yama told her to go back; she spoke of dharma and the friendship of the good. Pleased, he granted
  boons, anything but Satyavan's life: (1) sight and strength for her father-in-law, (2) his lost kingdom, (3) a hundred
  sons for her father, (4) a hundred sons for herself by Satyavan (281).
- She then said: you have granted me a hundred sons, yet you take away my husband; let Satyavan live. Yama, pleased, set
  him free and said he would live with her four hundred years (281). Satyavan woke as if from sleep (282).
- Iconography, no plot claim: Yama's buffalo (his vahana) and mace (Vishnudharmottara, temple images).
- Vat Savitri: married women fast and worship the banyan for their husbands' lives, in Savitri's name (the vrata
  tradition, e.g. Skanda Purana); shown only as today's ritual, not as the story's setting.
- Performance only, no plot claim: Yama's sweat in the hook, his absent-minded wave and the frozen take at "TATHASTU",
  his laugh, the wink, the babies in the bubble (a picture of the boon, not a scene from the text).
