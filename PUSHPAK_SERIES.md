# PUSHPAK: a 3D mini-series on Ravana's vimana

Owner, 24 Nov 2026: "a beautiful 3D explainer reel on Ravana's Pushpaka vimana. Recreate the vimana as per texts.
Explain the size, fun facts, what happened to it after Ram went on it to Ayodhya. Mini series if too long."
Then: "Save the plan. Do myth busts when applicable."

**Style:** 3D (the film look, matte gold, low bloom: see 3D_GUIDE.md and the "not too shiny" note on ad 64) + girish VO +
score + SFX. Three standalone parts of 60–75 s, posted on consecutive days, 1 → 2 → 3. Each ends with a "Part N+1" line.
**The hero:** one Pushpak model built from the verses below, shared on main as `src/cine3d_pushpak.js` (built on Part 1's
branch, merged to main), reused by all three parts. Figures stay small (silhouettes / figurines) so the vimana leads.
**Myth busts** wherever one applies (owner): each part carries at least one, stated plainly and sourced.

## Facts used (Valmiki Ramayana, Gita Press numbering; Sanskrit checked on heritage.iitk.ac.in/VRAM, Sundara/Yuddha also on valmikiramayan.net via the Wayback Machine)

**Made and given.** Made by Vishvakarma in heaven for Brahma; Kubera got it from Brahma by great tapas; Ravana took it
by defeating Kubera (5.9.11–12). Brahma to Kubera: "take this Pushpaka, like the sun, as your vehicle; be equal to
the gods" (7.3.19). Built by Vishvakarma "with his mind" (6.127.29).

**How it looked.**
- Gold pillars, arches of vaidurya (cat's-eye), covered with nets of pearls, trees bearing every wished-for fruit (7.15.39).
- Steps of gems and gold, platforms of molten gold (7.15.40); gold stairways (5.9.15); windows of gold and crystal,
  platforms of sapphire and emerald (5.9.16); floors of coral, gems and pearls (5.9.17).
- Artificial birds of cat's-eye, silver and coral; serpents of jewels; well-bred horses (5.7.12); birds with coral wings
  and golden flowers (5.7.13).
- Elephants in a lotus pool, lotus petals in their trunks, worshipping an image of four-armed Lakshmi holding a lotus (5.7.14).
- Many peaks, "like a mountain with wonderful peaks" (5.8.5); rows of upper rooms (5.9.19).
- Swan images: "provided with the image of a swan" (hamsayukta, 6.122.26; 6.123.1).
- Shining like the sun, gold parts, cat's-eye platform, attics, white flags and flagstaffs, golden lotuses, networks of
  tiny bells giving a melodious sound (6.121.23–28). Decorated with flowers (6.122.1).

**Size.** No dimension for the Pushpaka anywhere we read.
- "Half a yojana wide and a yojana long" is Ravana's palace (bhavana, 5.9.2). The Pushpaka stood in the middle of that
  building (5.8.1).
- It looked like a cloud (6.121.11) and like a mountain (6.121.30).
- Sugriva with the monkeys and Vibhishana with his ministers boarded (6.122.24); "all those mighty monkeys, bears and
  demons sat comfortably and spaciously" (yathāsukham asambādham, 6.122.27).
- kāmarūpa: it takes any form at will (7.15.40).

**How it flew.** manojava (speed of thought), kāmaga (goes anywhere at will), vihaṅgama (sky-goer) (7.15.40); "moves by the
thoughts of a concentrated mind" (5.8.4); carried by thousands of bhutas (5.8.6); indestructible (akṣayya, 7.15.41);
"neither cold nor hot, pleasant in every season" (7.15.42); rose "like a great cloud lifted by the wind" (6.123.1).

**Its story.**
- Kubera visits his parents in it (7.3.35; 7.9.40). Ravana's mother Kaikesi sees him blazing and tells Ravana "look at
  your brother… be like him" (7.9.41–43); Ravana vows and goes to Gokarna for tapas (7.9.44–47).
- Ravana defeats Kubera and takes the Pushpaka as a sign of victory (7.15.38); "thought he had conquered the three worlds" (7.15.43).
- Over the Sharavana reed forest it stops; Ravana asks why it won't go at his will (7.16.3–5); Maricha: "or this
  Pushpaka carries no one but Kubera, so without its master it stands still" (7.16.7); Nandi: "turn back, Shankara
  plays on this mountain" (7.16.10); Ravana: "the Pushpaka's course was cut because of this mountain… I'll uproot it" (7.16.23).
- Sita is flown over the battlefield; Trijata: "this Pushpaka would not have carried you if these two had lost their
  lives" (6.48.25); it is "swift as thought" (6.48.35).
- Vibhishana offers it to Ram: Kubera's, robbed by Ravana, "kept here for your sake" (6.121.9–10); Ram and Lakshman
  are amazed (6.121.30).
- The monkey wives walk round it clockwise before boarding at Kishkindha (6.123.36).
- At Nandigram Ram tells it: "carry Vaishravana; I permit you to go" (6.127.59); it flies north to Kubera (6.127.60–61).
- It comes back: a sweet voice from the sky, "O gentle Ram, look at me with a gentle face; know me as the Pushpaka,
  come from Kubera's palace" (7.41.2–3). Kubera told it: "you were won by Ram in Lanka; carry him" (7.41.7–8). "By
  Kubera's order I move unseen by all beings" (7.41.10). Ram welcomes it, worships it with puffed rice, flowers and
  incense (7.41.12–13): "go, and come when I think of you" (7.41.14).
- Ram thinks of it, it comes within a muhurta: "here I am, your servant" (7.75.5–7); he flies to Agastya's ashram in it
  (7.76.19–28) and back, then sends it off: "go, may you be well" (7.82.19). No mention in 7.83–111 (we scanned to verse
  60 of each sarga).

**The Vaimanika Shastra** (Wikipedia, citing Mukunda et al., "A critical study of the work Vyamanika Shastra",
*Scientific Opinion*, 1974, IISc Bangalore): dictated by Pandit Subbaraya Shastry in 1918–1923 (claimed as channelled from
the sage Bharadvaja), announced by G. R. Josyer in 1952, printed in Hindi 1959 and English 1973. IISc: "None of the planes
has properties or capabilities of being flown."
